## Korea

### 1. Yoon and Kim Keon-hee face each other in court

- **Date**: Apr 13–14 (Mon–Tue)
- **What happened**: On April 14, Criminal Division 33 of the Seoul Central District Court (presiding judge Lee Jin-kwan) called Kim Keon-hee as a witness in the trial of former President Yoon Suk Yeol and political broker Myung Tae-kyun on charges of violating the Political Funds Act. It was the first time the two had faced each other in the same courtroom since Yoon was arrested again last July and Kim was arrested in August.

  The special counsel team led by Min Joong-ki presented recordings of calls and messages with Myung and asked whether she had received polls for free. Kim answered only “Yes” when asked whether she was Yoon’s spouse and refused to answer every other question. The questioning ended after a little over 30 minutes. The court said it would close the arguments on May 12.

  Yoon is charged with conspiring with Kim to receive 58 polls worth 270 million won from Myung for free between June 2021 and March 2022. Kim was charged separately with the same offense, was found not guilty at the first trial, and is awaiting the appeals court’s ruling. On the 13th, the court had told Kim, who appeared as a witness in the insurrection trial of former Justice Minister Park Sung-jae, to take off her mask, and on the 14th it explained, “For a person whose statements must be judged for credibility, attitude and facial expressions are also used in making that judgment.”
- **Why it matters**: On the charge that Yoon conspired with Kim, the special counsel did not get any testimony from her. Kim, charged separately with the same offense, was found not guilty at the first trial on the grounds that she could not be seen as having gained a financial benefit, and the appeals ruling comes on the 28th. Yoon’s trial is set to close its arguments on May 12, with a ruling expected in June.

### 2. President Lee pledges “real contribution” at Hormuz summit

- **Date**: Apr 17–18 (Fri–Sat)
- **What happened**: On April 17, President Lee Jae-myung took part in a video summit on freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz, co-hosted by Britain and France, and said, “We will make a real contribution to guaranteeing freedom of navigation in the strait.” Lee said, “Korea is a key stakeholder that imports about 70% of its crude oil through the Strait of Hormuz,” and that it remained hard to guarantee the safety of Korean seafarers stuck inside the strait. The countries at the meeting agreed to increase diplomatic and military cooperation to keep shipping safe in the strait after the war ends. If a Europe-led multinational force is formed, the Korean military is considering taking part, with priority on indirect contributions such as sharing intelligence and providing logistical support.

  On the same day, the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries said a Korean ship carrying crude oil from the Saudi port of Yanbu had safely made it out of the Red Sea. It was the first time crude oil had been brought to Korea by way of the Red Sea since the Strait of Hormuz was blocked. Twenty-six Korean ships are stranded inside the strait, and on the 18th it was reported that a Korean ship leaving Qatar planned to become the first to exit the strait with permission from Iranian authorities.

  On the 18th (local time), a spokesperson for Iran’s foreign ministry criticized the Europe-led initiative that Korea joined, saying it had turned into extreme hypocrisy.
- **Why it matters**: With the strait that most of Korea’s oil imports pass through blocked for nearly 50 days, the government said it would take part in a multinational initiative for maritime security and actually started using a detour route. How Korea would take part in a multinational force is still under review, and Iran sees the initiative as hostile.

### 3. Court rejects arrest of YouTuber Jeon Han-gil

- **Date**: Apr 16 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On April 16, the Seoul Central District Court rejected an arrest warrant for YouTuber Jeon Han-gil, who is accused of defaming President Lee Jae-myung and Reform Party leader Lee Jun-seok. The court said, “It is hard to see a risk that he would destroy evidence or flee.”

  Jeon is accused of claiming on YouTube that President Lee had built up a slush fund of 160 trillion won, and of saying that Lee Jun-seok’s double major in economics at Harvard was a lie. After analyzing his donation accounts, the police found that Jeon had earned 32.6 million won from six videos with these claims and charged him with violating the Framework Act on Telecommunications. Prosecutors requested the warrant, arguing that he was likely to offend again because he had repeatedly made and spread fake news.

  As he was released, Jeon said, “The courts and the people have passed judgment on the Lee Jae-myung government’s excessive complaints and investigations.” The police plan to study the reasons for the rejection, investigate further complaints and then consider applying for a warrant again.
- **Why it matters**: The police and prosecutors saw the false claims about the sitting president and an opposition party leader as a repeated crime for profit that required detention, but the court did not accept the grounds for arrest. Jeon will be investigated without being detained, and the police will review again whether to seek his detention.

### 4. Seoul mayor race set: Oh Se-hoon vs. Jung Won-o

- **Date**: Apr 18 (Sat)
- **What happened**: On April 18, the People Power Party confirmed Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon as its candidate for mayor in the June 3 local elections. Oh beat lawmaker Park Soo-min and Yoon Hee-suk, former head of the party’s think tank, the Yeouido Institute, in a primary on the 16th and 17th that weighted a public poll and a vote of party members equally. In a Gallup Korea poll (April 10–11) released on the 13th, Jung had 52% and Oh 37%. With many sitting lawmakers running for mayor or governor, the National Assembly by-elections held alongside the local elections will also be larger.

### 5. Samsung asks court to ban union strike action

- **Date**: Apr 16–17 (Thu–Fri)
- **What happened**: On April 16, Samsung Electronics asked the Suwon District Court for an injunction banning its unions from industrial action. The unions have announced a rally in Pyeongtaek on the 23rd, to be followed by an 18-day general strike from May 21 to June 7. The company said it was worried that a strike could cause safety accidents such as chemical leaks and fires, losses from damaged equipment and discarded materials, and disruptions to chip supply.

  The two sides remain far apart on the bonus pool. The company proposed putting more than 10% of operating profit into the pool if it posts the best results in Korea, which is estimated to mean an average bonus of 540 million won for each memory division employee. The unions demanded 15% of operating profit.

  At a press conference in front of Samsung’s Seocho office on the 17th, the Super-Enterprise Union said it had secured official status as the union representing a majority of Samsung Electronics workers and as the workers’ representative. Choi Seung-ho, the union’s head, said an 18-day strike would cost the company at least 20 trillion to 30 trillion won, including the cost of backing up equipment.
- **Why it matters**: Samsung Electronics now has a majority union for the first time, and the company has turned to legal action ahead of a strike. By the unions’ calculation, at a company whose operating profit this year is forecast at about 300 trillion won, a general strike could cause about 1 trillion won in lost production each day.

## World

### 1. U.S. begins naval blockade of Iranian ports

- **Date**: Apr 13–19 (Mon–Sun)
- **What happened**: After talks between the U.S. and Iran in Islamabad, Pakistan, ended without a deal, the U.S. military began a naval blockade of ships going to and from Iranian ports on April 13. U.S. President Donald Trump wrote on social media that he would blockade all ships going in and out of the Strait of Hormuz, but the blockade the U.S. military announced was limited to Iranian ports. By the 18th, more than 20 ships had been turned back.

  On the 17th, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi announced that all merchant ships could pass through the strait on a set route during the ceasefire in Lebanon. Trump welcomed it as a great day for the world but said the blockade would continue until a peace deal was reached. On the 18th, Iran said it would put the strait back under “strict control” because the U.S. blockade was continuing, and on the same day an Indian ship came under fire in the strait.

  On the 19th, the U.S. Navy seized an Iranian cargo ship that was trying to break through the blockade. Trump said he would send a delegation led by Vice President JD Vance to Islamabad, but Iranian state media said Iran had no plans to take part in the next round of talks. The ceasefire between the U.S. and Iran expires on the 22nd.
- **Why it matters**: The strait, which carried about 20% of the world’s oil and liquefied natural gas, has been all but closed since the war began on February 28. With the U.S. answering with a blockade and Iran reversing its decision to open the strait, both the reopening of the strait and the talks to end the war became tied to whether the blockade is lifted. More than 3,300 people have been killed in Iran alone.

### 2. Israel and Lebanon agree 10-day ceasefire after direct talks

- **Date**: Apr 14–16 (Tue–Thu)
- **What happened**: On April 14, Israel and Lebanon held their first direct diplomatic talks in decades at the State Department in Washington. The two countries’ ambassadors to the U.S. sat across from each other, joined by Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Rubio said the talks were meant to lay the groundwork for a lasting peace. Hezbollah leader Naim Qassem opposed the talks, calling them pointless.

  On the 16th, after separate phone calls with Lebanese President Joseph Aoun and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, U.S. President Donald Trump announced a 10-day ceasefire, which took effect at midnight that day. The terms announced by the State Department bar Israel from offensive actions in Lebanon but allow “self-defense” against attacks that are about to happen. Netanyahu said disarming Hezbollah was a precondition and that Israel would keep a 10 km “security zone” along the border. A Hezbollah lawmaker said the group would respect the ceasefire as long as Israel stopped its hostile acts.

  After the ceasefire took effect, thousands of displaced people began returning to villages in the south that lay in ruins.
- **Why it matters**: In this conflict, which began with a Hezbollah attack on March 2, more than 2,100 people have been killed on the Lebanese side, and Israel invaded southern Lebanon by land. Trump said Aoun and Netanyahu would meet in Washington the following week. The ceasefire is also tied to the U.S.–Iran talks to end the war.

### 5. IAEA says North Korea’s nuclear capacity has grown sharply

- **Date**: Apr 15 (Wed)
- **What happened**: In Seoul on April 15, Rafael Grossi, Director General of the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), confirmed that activity at North Korea’s Yongbyon nuclear complex had increased quickly. Grossi said work had stepped up at the 5 MW reactor, the reprocessing facility and the light-water reactor, among others, and that North Korea’s capacity to produce nuclear weapons had grown very seriously. North Korea is estimated to have assembled about 50 nuclear warheads.

  In the same week, Beyond Parallel, a North Korea analysis site run by the U.S. Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS), said based on satellite images that a new suspected uranium enrichment facility at Yongbyon was close to being ready to operate. This facility and the one at Kangson near Pyongyang have not been declared to international nuclear authorities.
- **Why it matters**: If the uranium enrichment facility starts operating, the number of nuclear weapons North Korea can have will rise sharply. Last June, the IAEA also assessed that an enrichment facility able to produce weapons-grade material was being built at Yongbyon.

### 3. Anthropic unveils design tool Claude Design

- **Date**: Apr 17 (Fri)
- **What happened**: On April 17, Anthropic released Claude Design as a research preview, a new product for creating design mockups, prototypes, slides, one-page documents and more through conversation. Users describe what they want, Claude makes a first version, and users refine it through chat, inline comments, direct edits and adjustment sliders. It reads a company’s codebase and design files to build the team’s design system and applies it automatically to all later work.

  Results can be exported as PDF, URL or PPTX, or sent to Canva for editing. It runs on the new model Claude Opus 4.7 and is available to Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise subscribers.
- **Why it matters**: The product lets founders and product managers with no design background turn ideas into visuals right away, and with it Anthropic is moving beyond coding tools into tools for office work. Canva also expanded its AI assistant the same week, and Anthropic said Claude Design adds to Canva rather than replacing it.

### 5. Anthropic releases Claude Opus 4.7

- **Date**: Apr 16 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On April 16, Anthropic released Claude Opus 4.7, its most powerful model available to the public. It does better than Opus 4.6 on difficult software development tasks, sees images at higher resolution and follows instructions more precisely. It costs $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens, the same as Opus 4.6.

  Opus 4.7 scores lower than Mythos Preview, which is offered privately, on every relevant evaluation. Anthropic had said it would limit the release of Mythos and test new cybersecurity safeguards first on less capable models, and it said Opus 4.7 is the first such model. It automatically detects and blocks banned or high-risk cybersecurity requests, and Anthropic also experimented with selectively reducing its ability to carry out cyberattacks during training. Security professionals with legitimate purposes, such as vulnerability research, can use it by joining a new Cyber Verification Program.
- **Why it matters**: Anthropic said it will use the results of running these safeguards in practice as the basis for moving toward a public release of Mythos-class models. Rather than releasing its strongest model, it is releasing a model one level down with safeguards first.
