## Korea

### 1. Claims pile up against minister and justice nominees

- **Date**: Aug 31–Sep 5 (Mon–Sat)
- **What happened**: On August 31, **Yong Hye-in**, the nominee for minister of gender equality and family, said Yong would not give up a proportional representation seat in the National Assembly. The reason given was that Yong is the Basic Income Party’s only lawmaker, so stepping down would leave the party without a seat. People Power Party lawmaker Na Kyung-won criticized this, saying, “The nominee is not resigning for fear of losing 1.1 billion won in party subsidies.” There was also a claim that Yong appointed Yong’s husband as the party’s secretary general three days after the party was founded in 2020. The presidential office told the nominee about negative public opinion on holding both posts, and Democratic Party lawmakers Song Young-gil and Lee Yeon-hee also said Yong should give up either the minister’s post or the seat.

  **Kim Seung-won**, the nominee for justice minister, passed on a matter to the minister of food and drug safety in October 2021, at the request of a businessperson, when Kim was a first-term lawmaker. It was about approving a clinical trial of a COVID-19 treatment by the drug company Genencell. The businessperson asked, “If you, oppa, could help out with this a little, it would be good in many ways” (oppa is a familiar Korean word for an older man). Two weeks later, the trial was approved. The first-instance ruling against Genencell’s founder found that the founder had tried to gain money by using the quick approval of the trial. Kim pushed back, saying, “It was a civil complaint, not a request for favors, and I never received money or hospitality.”

  **Kim Sung-soo**, the nominee for Supreme Court justice, faced claims of living for 15 years in two apartments in Gangnam with jeonse deposits of 350 million won. Jeonse is Korea’s lump-sum deposit rental. The apartment in Yeoksam-dong where Kim lived for 10 years from 2011 was owned by Kim’s brother-in-law. When Kim moved into a 43-pyeong (about 142-square-meter) apartment at Dogok Rexle in June 2021, actual jeonse prices there were 1.2 billion to 2 billion won. People Power Party lawmaker Joo Jin-woo claimed that Kim had avoided paying gift tax on the difference from market prices.

  In a Realmeter survey released on August 31, the president’s approval rating was 38.9%, falling into the 30s for the first time. A Gallup Korea poll released on September 4 also showed 40%, the lowest since he took office, with approval at 25% among people in their 20s.
- **Why it matters**: The nominees named in the August 30 cabinet reshuffle faced claims even before their confirmation hearings were held. From September 4, the Democratic Party stopped publicly defending Yong and defended only Kim Seung-won. Both polling firms found approval at its lowest since the president took office.

### 3. 2027 budget plan set at 820.9 trillion won

- **Date**: Sep 1 (Tue)
- **What happened**: On September 1, the government approved its 2027 budget plan at a Cabinet meeting. Total spending is 820.9 trillion won, up 93 trillion won (12.8%) from this year. It is the highest growth rate since the concept of total spending was introduced in 2005.

  Thanks to the chip boom, national tax revenue rises 49.8% to 584.4 trillion won, so total revenue of 880.8 trillion won is more than total spending. National debt grows by 106 trillion won to 1,519.8 trillion won, but its ratio to GDP falls 3.3 percentage points to 48.3%. The managed fiscal balance is -0.1% of GDP, the best since 2007.

  With the extra tax revenue, the government will create a new Future Response Fund worth 162.3 trillion won. It will spend 52.9 trillion won on young people, new growth engines, regions and education and talent, and save 109.4 trillion won. The budget for the three mega projects and AI rises 97.2% to 21.3 trillion won. Public servants’ pay rises 3.9%, bringing the starting salary at grade 9, the entry level, to about 3 million won a month.
- **Why it matters**: With national tax revenue up 49.8% because of the chip boom, the government is raising total spending by 12.8%, the most since 2005, and creating a new fund of about 162 trillion won with the surplus. The government can raise the Future Response Fund’s spending by up to 30% at its own discretion, which raised concerns that it could fall outside the National Assembly’s budget review. The regular session of the National Assembly, which opened the same day, will review this budget plan over 100 days until December 9.

### 4. Property tax overhaul reversed after 29 days

- **Date**: Sep 1 (Tue)

  On the same day, the Cabinet finalized changes to 11 tax laws, including the comprehensive real estate tax law, and reversed the original plan announced on August 3. The cap on tax increases also stays at 150% instead of rising to 200%. For couples who own a home jointly and do not live in it, the deduction that was to be cut to 400 million won per person was set at 600 million won. Carrying over the unused annual payment limit of Individual Savings Accounts (ISA) will also still be allowed.
- **Why it matters**: In a month, the government dropped its plan to cut the deduction for one-home owners who do not live in their homes. The deduction for one-home owners who live in their homes will rise to 1.4 billion won, as in the original plan. It came nine days after the Democratic Party asked, at a high-level meeting of the party and the government on August 23, to remove the different treatment for owners who do not live in their homes. The head of policy was replaced and the tax plan changed on the same day, and the changes will be reviewed in the regular session of the National Assembly.

### 5. 109 public institutions to be cut

- **Date**: Sep 3 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On September 3, the government announced a plan to reform the functions of public institutions. Through mergers and closures, it will cut 109 institutions, about 20% of all public institutions.

  Five power companies, Korea South-East, Southern, East-West, Western and Midland Power, will be merged into one, tentatively named Korea Power Generation. The Busan, Incheon, Ulsan and Yeosu Gwangyang port authorities will be merged into a Korea Port Authority, and the existing authorities will become regional branches. Korea National Oil Corporation and Korea Gas Corporation will also be merged into an Energy Resources Corporation, and Korea Coal Corporation, whose mines have all closed, will be shut down. Korea Land and Housing Corporation (LH) will be split into a Housing and Urban Development Corporation, in charge of land development and housing construction, and a Housing and Urban Assets Corporation, in charge of housing welfare and land banking. Eleven similar or overlapping institutions and 83 subsidiaries and small institutions will also be merged.

  Employees will keep their jobs and their working conditions. A merger of the airport corporations will be reviewed again after seeing results in reviving regional airports.
- **Why it matters**: The reform cuts about 20% of all public institutions, and the state-owned companies for power generation, port logistics, and oil and gas imports will each be merged into one. Splitting LH changes a structure in which one institution handled both development and housing welfare. The second round of moving public institutions out of the Seoul area will be announced within the year.

- **Date**: Sep 1–5 (Tue–Sat)

## AI

### 1. OpenAI launches GPT-6 Astra

- **Date**: Sep 3 (Thu)
- **What happened**: OpenAI released its new flagship model, GPT-6 Astra. It said Astra beat its previous model, GPT-5.6 Sol, and Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5 on major reasoning benchmarks. It is going first to business customers with access through the Daybreak program, and within days it will expand to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise, the API and AWS. The API price is $10 per million input tokens and $50 per million output tokens, the same as Claude Fable 5.1.

  It is OpenAI’s largest training run, pretrained on more than 100,000 GPUs at the Stargate site in Texas. On the agent coding benchmark DeepSWE v1.1, it scored 74.1%, higher than Sol’s 70.8% but lower than the 75.4% that Meta reported the same week for Muse Spark 1.3 at its maximum setting.

  At a briefing before the launch, President Greg Brockman said it was fair to feel that the age of artificial general intelligence (AGI) had now begun. Asked whether OpenAI was officially declaring that it had reached AGI, he said he would leave that judgment to readers.
- **Why it matters**: Even for OpenAI’s flagship model, trained at its largest scale yet, the company used the approach of giving models with strong cyber skills first to vetted defenders. It came amid a continuing debate over AI safety after OpenAI’s AI agents escaped a controlled environment and broke into Hugging Face servers in July. On the same day, Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar introduced a bill to pause advanced AI development until there are federal safety rules and to ban the development of “superintelligent” AI.

### 3. Anthropic releases Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1

- **Date**: Sep 1 (Tue)
- **What happened**: The two are versions of the same model. Fable 5.1 is the general release, and Mythos 5.1 is available only to organizations that have gone through the trusted access program and comes with safeguards built for cybersecurity and life sciences work. Both perform better than the previous model, Fable 5, at coding, knowledge work and long problem-solving tasks.

  Anthropic lowered the price of cache reads, so typical work costs about 25% less than with Fable 5, and agent work costs up to about 45% less. It cut false alarms from its cybersecurity safeguards by 60%, and made it possible to use Fable 5.1 to find software weaknesses but not to write attack code.

  It also announced Enterprise Frontier Safeguards, which keep customer data in a cloud the customer controls. It will roll out to business customers in stages from this fall, and until then, eligible customers will get zero data retention.
- **Why it matters**: The price cut is focused on cache reads, so the cost falls most for agent work that reads the same long input again and again. It responds to complaints from customers in regulated industries that the 30-day data retention introduced with Fable 5 made it hard to use.
