## Korea

### 1. U.S. commander says OPCON transfer conditions changed

- **Date**: Oct 13–18 (Tue–Sun)
- **What happened**: On October 14 (local time), Gen. Xavier Brunson, commander of U.S. Forces Korea and the Combined Forces Command, spoke at a seminar of the Gold Institute for International Strategy in Washington. On the transfer of wartime operational control (OPCON), he said, “The conditions have changed, and because we changed our plans, there is a new standard we must meet.” He pointed to North Korea’s missile launches. Earlier, in a talk at the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS), he said he supports only a “conditions-based” transfer. He said he would follow the president if told to carry it out now, but would clearly report the risks of doing so before the conditions are fully met. In the same talk, he made official the deployment of a U.S. Army Multi-Domain Task Force (MDTF) to the Korean Peninsula. He said a single line on a map shows the distance from Beijing to Seoul. The U.S. reportedly judged that some Full Operational Capability (FOC) items it passed last year were not met this year.

  The Defense Ministry pushed back. Defense Minister Kang Shin-cheol told a parliamentary audit on the 15th that he had conveyed concern and a protest to the U.S. Defense Ministry spokesperson Jeong Bit-na said, “If the goalposts keep moving, I think the OPCON transfer can never happen.” At the seminar on the 14th, Brunson also said, “From the UN Command’s point of view, the Republic of Korea begins at that southern fence line.” The remark set off a dispute over territorial sovereignty. On the 18th, the Defense Ministry called it “the personal view of the UN Command commander” and said its position on territorial sovereignty is firm. The UN Command also said, “There is no question about the sovereignty of the Republic of Korea.” The U.S. administration reportedly told Seoul that it had cautioned Brunson after the Korean protest.

  At a Cabinet meeting on the 13th, President Lee Jae-myung said, “In relations between countries, we must avoid a submissive or subservient attitude.” He stressed pragmatic diplomacy for the national interest. This came as debate continued over a rift in the alliance, including U.S. limits on sharing intelligence on North Korea.
- **Why it matters**: The U.S. commander who assesses the OPCON transfer is now asking for a new standard on top of the agreed conditions. This makes it more likely that the government’s plan to complete the transfer by 2028 will be delayed. The U.S. side wants a Korean commander to run advanced U.S. command-and-control systems directly after the transfer, including AI-based target analysis and intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance (ISR). It reportedly sees a transfer within one to two years as difficult. The presidential office is reportedly seeking a “top-down” solution at a senior level. On the MDTF deployment, there are worries that Korea will be tied more closely to the U.S. military’s China strategy.

### 2. KOSPI falls 20% in seven days, circuit breakers triggered

- **Date**: Oct 12–16 (Mon–Fri)
- **What happened**: On October 15, the KOSPI fell 7.57% to close at 5,554.22. It fell as much as 9.29% during the day, and level-one circuit breakers stopped trading for 20 minutes on both the main board and the KOSDAQ. It was the first time since July 29 that both markets triggered circuit breakers on the same day. The KOSDAQ fell 7.65%. Samsung Electronics fell 9.48% and SK Hynix fell 11.06%. Foreign investors were net sellers of 3.785 trillion won on the main board that day. The KOSPI had already fallen more than 3% on the 12th and 5.46% on the 13th, to the low 6,000s, and a sell-side sidecar was triggered on the 13th.

  The KOSPI fell for seven straight trading days from the 6th to the 15th, losing 1,449.52 points (20.70%). It rebounded on the 16th to close at 5,668.05, but lost 14.46% over the week. Compared with June 22, when it hit a record high of 9,114.55, its market value has shrunk by 2,869 trillion won.

  The fall came from worries over U.S. inflation and the returns on AI investment. On October 14 (local time), the U.S. Labor Department said consumer prices rose 3.7% in September, above the expected 3.6%. This raised the chance of an interest rate hike by the Federal Reserve. The price of bonds issued by AI cloud company CoreWeave also plunged, spreading doubts about AI companies’ debt and returns.
- **Why it matters**: Measured over seven trading days, the KOSPI has fallen further only three times: in the December 1997 currency crisis (−25%), the October 2008 global financial crisis (−31%) and the March 2020 COVID-19 shock (−25%). On the 15th, the KOSPI’s fall was 2.2 times that of Taiwan’s TAIEX (3.41%) and 1.5 times that of Japan’s Nikkei (4.90%). Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix make up 49.71% of the KOSPI’s market value. Experts warn that if the chip cycle turns, growth, exports and tax revenue will all be hit.

### 3. 44 bodies, including foreign and defense, to move to Sejong

- **Date**: Oct 13–16 (Tue–Fri)
- **What happened**: On October 13, the government announced that it will move 44 central government bodies and affiliated agencies in the Seoul area to Sejong by 2031. They include the Foreign Ministry, the Unification Ministry, the Defense Ministry and the Financial Services Commission. A total of 9,929 officials, counted by authorized staff numbers, will move. The 27 bodies that need no separate building, with 2,995 officials, will move into rented private offices in Sejong during 2027. Six will move in the first half, including the Korea Media and Communications Commission, the Personal Information Protection Commission, the Nuclear Safety and Security Commission and the National Human Rights Commission. Another 19, including the Financial Services Commission and agencies under ministries, will move in the second half. Seventeen bodies with 6,934 officials will move by 2031 after new buildings are built. They include the Board of Audit and Inspection, the National Police Agency, the Corruption Investigation Office for High-ranking Officials, the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency and the foreign, unification and defense ministries.

  The foreign, unification and defense ministries, along with the Justice Ministry and the Ministry of Gender Equality and Family, are excluded from relocation under the law on Sejong’s multifunctional administrative city. The law must be amended. The government estimates about 89.7 billion won next year for rented offices and relocation support, and about 3.1 trillion won to build new offices.

  Critics said the plan was rushed. On October 16, it emerged that the Interior Ministry had told a lawmaker on September 11 that it had “never reviewed” moving the foreign and unification ministries. At the Cabinet meeting on the 13th, Financial Services Commission Chairman Lee Eog-weon reportedly said there were “not a few voices of concern” about moving the commission. Whether the Joint Chiefs of Staff will move has not been decided. Critics say that if only the Defense Ministry moves, defense policy will sit in Sejong and operational command in Seoul.
- **Why it matters**: The plan moves even the foreign affairs and security ministries that the law has kept out of Sejong, to bring a government split between Sejong and the Seoul area together around Sejong. The presidential office reportedly plans for President Lee Jae-myung to work in Sejong one or two days a week once his office there is finished. The Foreign Ministry is worried that it will be more than 100 km from 118 foreign embassies in Seoul and 27 international organization offices in the Seoul area.

### 4. Tax chief Lim Kwang-hyun named presidential chief of staff

- **Date**: Oct 14 (Wed)
- **What happened**: On October 14, President Lee Jae-myung appointed Lim Kwang-hyun, 57, commissioner of the National Tax Service, as his new chief of staff. Lim said, “I will give all my strength to results for people’s livelihoods.” His brother-in-law is lawyer Lee Seung-yeop, who was on President Lee’s defense teams in his election law case and in the Ssangbangwool case over alleged illegal payments to North Korea. Jeong Jeom-sik, floor leader of the People Power Party, criticized the choice as “an appointment that shows the president has no sense at all of the crisis in state affairs.”
- **Why it matters**: The choice is seen as a sign that President Lee is putting policy delivery first, choosing a tax commissioner with long administrative experience over a political aide. A senior presidential official said, “We chose a person who can support the administration based on results.” The People Power Party questioned whether Lim can untangle state affairs such as housing, stocks, North Korea and relations with the U.S.

### 5. Firefighter dies in Bucheon fire; governor’s remark angers

- **Date**: Oct 13–18 (Tue–Sun)
- **What happened**: On October 16, a fire broke out at a metal parts painting factory in Ojeong-gu, Bucheon, Gyeonggi Province. Six firefighters and two workers were hurt. Firefighter Kim Jin-seok, 44, was trapped deep inside the third floor when the fire suddenly grew. He was rescued in cardiac arrest and died at around 0:55 a.m. on the 17th. He was in the first crew, which arrived five minutes after the call. He had a daughter who will start elementary school next year. The government promoted him one rank, from fire sergeant to fire lieutenant, and awarded him the Order of Service Merit, Okjo Medal. About 180 20-liter containers of flammable liquid used to clean metal were found at the factory. The factory had no permit to handle hazardous materials. On the 18th, police booked the factory’s owner on charges of negligence causing injury and accidental fire through negligence.

  A remark by Gyeonggi Governor Choo Mi-ae three days earlier then drew renewed anger. At a parliamentary audit on the 13th, she said, “Firefighting seems like the biggest, most troublesome budget straw.” She was explaining that firefighter wages alone will cost 1.1 trillion won in 2027. Firefighters’ unions said she had damaged their honor and demanded an apology, and the Democratic Party apologized first on the 16th. At the funeral altar on the 17th, Choo said, “Since you want an apology, I truly apologize with all my heart.” This drew more criticism. It also emerged that Gyeonggi Province had cut 3.45 billion won from the firefighting budget in a supplementary budget sent to the provincial council in August. This included 2.65 billion won for replacing old fire trucks.
- **Why it matters**: The case showed that firefighters became national civil servants in 2020, but their budget, including wages, still depends on local finances. Of this year’s 6.5129 trillion won in firefighter wages for provinces and cities, the national government pays 549.6 billion won (8.4%). Four firefighters, including Kim, have died at fire scenes this year. The Democratic Party said it will address the remaining problems in funding and working conditions so that the purpose of the switch to national status is met in the field.

### 1. Houthis hit Yemen’s presidential palace; UAE re-enters war

- **Date**: Oct 12–17 (Mon–Sat)
- **What happened**: On October 15, Yemen’s Houthi rebels fired five missiles at the al-Maashiq presidential palace in the southern city of Aden, killing five people and injuring 16. The palace is the seat of Yemen’s internationally recognized government. The prime minister and officials were safe. On the 16th, two Houthi missiles hit Aden airport, about 500 m from a passenger plane preparing to take off.

  Strikes between the Houthis and Saudi Arabia also continued. After the Saudi-led coalition struck a fuel depot near the capital, Sanaa, on the 13th, the Houthis hit a building in Saudi Aramco’s headquarters complex in Dhahran, in the east, with a missile. After an attack on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport killed 13 people on the 10th, Saudi Arabia stopped all flights at the airport until the 17th. The coalition said the airport had been attacked more than 20 times in 11 days, and that Houthi attacks had killed 17 people and injured more than 300.

  On the ground, the Houthis have seized more than 1,000 km of territory in a month through an offensive on the Red Sea coast since September. They have surrounded Taiz, a city of about one million people. The World Food Programme (WFP) warned that 800,000 people in Taiz are not getting aid. On October 17, there were reports that the United Arab Emirates (UAE), which left Yemen in January, had agreed to resume funding some government forces, with Saudi Arabia providing air cover. Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi called an emergency session of parliament to give the military powers to defend Saudi Arabia.
- **Why it matters**: It is the first concrete sign that Saudi Arabia and the UAE, which split over Yemen’s future, will work together again in the face of the Houthi advance. Front lines that had been frozen since a UN-brokered truce in 2022 are moving, and control of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait at the mouth of the Red Sea is at stake. ## AI

### 1. Meta releases Muse Wonder, its first frontier model

- **Date**: Oct 12–15 (Mon–Thu)
- **What happened**: On October 12, Meta’s Superintelligence Labs released two models, Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full. They are Meta’s first frontier models. Meta said its own tests showed both models scoring close to OpenAI’s GPT-6 Astra and Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.1 and Opus 5.5 on benchmarks. Wonder costs $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens on the Meta Model API, about the same as GPT-6 Sol or Opus 5.5. Wonder Full is not offered through the API and is only for Muse Code subscribers. Meta said it put special effort into “active memory,” the ability to remember what users share for a long time.

  On the 15th, Meta also released Muse Spark Open, an open-weight model. It is a mixture-of-experts (MoE) model with about 816 billion parameters in total, of which about 42 billion are used for each token. It takes text, images, audio and video as input and can handle up to one million tokens at once. Meta called it the most capable open-weight model from the U.S. It is designed to run on Macs with at least 1 terabyte of memory.
- **Why it matters**: It is the first top-tier model from Meta since it reorganized its AI work in August 2025 and spent billions of dollars. Google released Gemini 4 Argon the next day, so both companies have rejoined a frontier model race that OpenAI and Anthropic had been leading. Meta plans to use Wonder in its AI agent, Muse.

### 3. Character.AI files for bankruptcy, sells assets to Disney

- **Date**: Oct 12 (Mon)
- **What happened**: On October 12, Character.AI, a service where users chat with AI characters they create, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in the U.S. Its court filing listed assets and liabilities each of $50 million to $100 million. The company agreed to sell some assets to Disney for $20 million and give it a nonexclusive, perpetual license to its technology. Disney is the lead bidder in the sale and sets the baseline for other bids. The sale is expected to close in late November or early December, and the service, including paid subscriptions, will keep running until then. - **Why it matters**: One of the leading startups of the early generative AI boom has collapsed five years after its founding. ### 4. Major U.S. financial firms block outside AI agents

- **Date**: Oct 13–17 (Tue–Sat)
- **What happened**: Major U.S. financial firms are blocking outside AI agents that enter accounts to do tasks for customers. On an earnings call on October 13, JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon said the bank will not let just any outside agent into the company because it is too risky for clients and for the bank. The company confirmed it blocks outside AI agents from doing tasks for customers. Charles Schwab CEO Rick Wurster named Meta’s Muse and said the firm will not let clients give their password to Muse to act for them. He asked who is liable if something goes wrong. Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan said the bank is telling the AI industry that a person should approve payments until agents improve.

  On the 13th, American Express launched an “Intent Intelligence Framework” that records a customer’s purchase intent and has the customer confirm it before an AI agent pays. Blocks are also spreading beyond finance. Yelp only allows agents that pay for its data, and Delta Air Lines stops agents from booking tickets. Users of Meta’s Muse, xAI’s Grok and OpenAI’s Dots have reported their agents being blocked on major websites.
- **Why it matters**: OpenAI, Meta and Google are launching agents that book trips and shop for users. But sites that handle money and personal data are shutting them out over security and liability, which limits what agents can do. The financial firms say the reason is that there are no rules on who is responsible when an agent makes a bad transaction.

### 5. Anthropic gives outside evaluator METR inside access

- **Date**: Oct 13–15 (Tue–Thu)
- **What happened**: On October 15, Anthropic said it had begun giving six researchers from METR, a nonprofit that evaluates AI models, access close to that of employees. They get company laptops and office space and use internal tools and systems at about the same level as Anthropic’s own risk assessment teams. Apart from a few categories such as customer data, attorney-client privileged information and model architecture, staff are encouraged to share information with them as with a colleague. METR will publish its assessments of Anthropic’s risk levels, incidents and practices, and Anthropic cannot block publication. Anthropic can ask METR to redact information in set categories. METR can publicly say whether the redactions were justified, as long as it does not reveal what was redacted.

  Earlier, on the 13th, there was a report that OpenAI and Anthropic had considered tighter limits on what outside evaluators can publish, over fears of leaking trade secrets. OpenAI said redacting confidential information is standard practice and that evaluators can disclose major redactions. Anthropic said its partners control their own review and publishing process. On the same day, more than 400 researchers called in an open letter for AI companies to adopt high-assurance containment systems and for an international body to track containment failures.
- **Why it matters**: The step gives an evaluator both inside information and the right to publish, as calls for independent checks have grown since late August, with incidents of AI agents going beyond their instructions disclosed every week or two. Anthropic compared it to bank supervision, where examiners work inside large banks. CEO Dario Amodei proposed in September placing outside evaluators inside AI labs. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, Nvidia and xAI have signed a White House accord committing to independent review.
