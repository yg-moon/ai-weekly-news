## Korea

### 1. President Lee open to a shorter term if needed

- **Date**: Aug 13–14 (Thu–Fri)
- **What happened**: On August 14, President Lee Jae-myung wrote on X that his campaign pledge and consistent position was to amend the Constitution “to change the presidential term to a four-year term that can be served twice, consecutively or not, so that responsible politics through a midterm review is possible.” He wrote, “My official position during the 2022 presidential election was that I could accept a shorter term if it was needed for constitutional change, and I still think the same.” He also proposed giving the National Assembly authority over the Board of Audit and Inspection, the right to recommend the prime minister and some appointment powers. He also proposed adding the spirit of the May 18 Gwangju Uprising and the Bu-Ma Democratic Protests to the preamble of the Constitution. He said a dual executive system and a parliamentary system were “neither desirable nor possible given public opinion.”

  It started with golf meetings. On the 13th, People Power Party lawmaker Kim Tae-ho said Lee had said at a golf meeting in early July, “We must amend the Constitution even if it means shortening my term.” On the morning of the 14th, a presidential office official said, “A four-year presidential system that can be served twice, with stronger powers for the National Assembly, has the highest public acceptance.” But the official said this did not confirm what was said at the meeting. The People Power Party lawmakers who played golf with Lee or were invited are Joo Ho-young, Park Duk-heum, Kim Tae-ho, Shin Sung-bum and Choi Hyung-du, most of whom served on the party’s special committee on constitutional change.
- **Why it matters**: A constitutional amendment needs the votes of at least two-thirds of all lawmakers to pass the National Assembly, so it needs 10 or more of the People Power Party’s 109 lawmakers. Lee directly stated the direction for changing the power structure and his willingness to accept a shorter term. Constitutional change is the Lee Jae-myung government’s top policy task.

### 2. August 13 housing plan: 230,000 homes for Seoul area

- **Date**: Aug 13 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On August 13, the government announced a plan to supply 230,000 more homes in the Seoul metropolitan area, together with a package of financial measures. It is the Lee Jae-myung government’s third housing supply plan.

  On supply, 27,000 homes will be built in three new public housing districts: 21,700 on the Korea University Deokso Farm greenbelt in Namyangju, 4,500 near the station in Jangji-dong, Gwangju, Gyeonggi Province, and 1,000 at Yeomchang Park in Gangseo-gu, Seoul. The government will announce more districts with 73,000 homes within the year, and aims to start construction at Taereung Country Club in September 2029 and at Gwacheon Racecourse in December 2029. To prevent speculation, all of Seoul’s greenbelt and the greenbelt in Seoul-area cities next to Seoul will be made land transaction permit zones. A government-wide meeting chaired by the prime minister to check on fast housing supply will also be set up. Minister of Land, Infrastructure and Transport Kim Yun-duk said the president’s order was to supply housing “so much that people might think, ‘Has the Lee Jae-myung government gone crazy building houses?’”

  Loans also change from January next year. Some one-home owners will not be able to take out or extend a loan for the jeonse home they live in. This applies to owners of an apartment in the Seoul area or a regulated area who rent that home out and have never lived in it with their spouse. Jeonse is Korea’s lump-sum deposit rental. The Financial Services Commission estimated these loans at about 9.3 trillion won in 60,000 cases. The guarantee ratio for jeonse loans to one-home owners will fall from 80% to 70% in the Seoul area and regulated areas, and from 90% to 80% elsewhere. For borrowers whose income rose more than 20%, the debt service ratio (DSR) will be calculated with their average income over the last two years, and for those whose income rose more than 30%, over the last three years.
- **Why it matters**: From next year, people will no longer be able to buy a home with a tenant’s jeonse deposit while living elsewhere on a jeonse loan themselves. It will also be harder to raise loan limits with income that jumped in one year, such as bonuses at chip companies. In a Gallup Korea poll from August 11 to 13, approval of President Lee’s performance was 44%, the lowest since he took office, and real estate policy was the top reason for disapproval (30%).

### 3. Firefighters mobilized nationwide for drought in the south

- **Date**: Aug 11–16 (Tue–Sun)
- **What happened**: On the night of August 11, the National Fire Agency issued a national fire mobilization order for drought areas in South Gyeongsang Province. It sent 200 water tank trucks and 400 firefighters from fire services in other provinces and cities, and from the 12th to 6 p.m. on the 13th they delivered 12,445 tons of water to farmland and reservoirs. It is the second time a national fire mobilization order has been issued for a drought, after Gangneung in August 2025. Military water trucks and the Korea Forest Service also helped supply water.

  As of the 13th, all 18 cities and counties in South Gyeongsang Province were at drought levels for farm water. Five of them, Miryang, Geoje, Haman, Uiryeong and Changnyeong, were at the highest “serious” level. The average reservoir level in South Gyeongsang was 32.8%, less than half the normal 70.8%, and crops died on about 2,400 ha of farmland, among other damage. In North Gyeongsang Province, the level of Unmun Dam, a water source for Daegu, Gyeongsan and other places, fell to 24.7%, making it the only water supply dam in the country at the “serious” level. Twenty-seven islands and villages in Tongyeong, Miryang, Yangsan, Hadong and Geoje had limited water supply or got water by supply ship and bottled water. From July 12 to August 11, rainfall in Busan, Ulsan and South Gyeongsang was 16.6 mm, 6.1% of the normal amount.

  On the 15th and 16th, a low-pressure system left after Typhoon Dolphin weakened brought rain to the south. By the afternoon of the 16th, 201.3 mm had fallen in Haenam, South Jeolla Province, and Mokpo got 66.4 mm in one hour, a record for August. The South Gyeongsang reservoir level rose 0.7 percentage points, and the National Fire Agency lifted the national fire mobilization order on the 15th. With heavy rain falling on dry ground, the Korea Forest Service raised the landslide alert for Busan, Ulsan, South Jeolla and Gwangju, and South Gyeongsang to “caution.”
- **Why it matters**: With a month’s rainfall at only 6% of normal, all of South Gyeongsang Province went into drought, and 27 islands and villages had to rely on limited water supply, water ships or bottled water. For the second year in a row, firefighting forces from across the country were gathered to supply farm water. The rain that ended the drought caused flooding in Mokpo.

- **Why it matters**: The boy went to a police station himself to report abuse, but he was neither separated from his grandmother nor protected by a court order, and he died three days later. ### 5. SK’s Chey Tae-won appeals divorce ruling again

- **Date**: Aug 14–16 (Fri–Sun)
- **What happened**: At about 11:59 p.m. on August 14, SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won appealed again to the Supreme Court against a retrial ruling. The ruling ordered him to pay 944 billion won in cash to Roh Soh-yeong, director of Art Center Nabi, as a division of property in their divorce. He filed about one minute before the deadline. Chey’s side issued a statement saying, “We will keep the negative impact on shareholders and group management to a minimum.”

- **Why it matters**: The case, which began with a request for divorce mediation in 2017, has gone back to the Supreme Court. The main view in the legal community is that the result is unlikely to be reversed, because the Supreme Court judges only points of law and the retrial followed the Supreme Court’s earlier ruling. With the new appeal, Chey avoids late interest and gains time to find a way to raise the cash.

### 1. Magnitude 7.4 earthquake in Colombia kills 265

- **Date**: Aug 10 (Mon)
- **What happened**: On August 10, a magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck near San José del Palmar in Chocó, western Colombia. It was 107 km deep, and the Colombian Geological Service said it was the strongest earthquake to hit Colombia this century. Western cities such as Cali, Pereira, Quibdó and Manizales were badly damaged, and shaking was also felt in Ecuador and Panama.

  On the evening of the 12th, President Abelardo de la Espriella said at least 265 people had died. The official number of missing is close to 500, but a database of missing people set up by citizens lists more than 4,100. More than 25,800 households were affected. By the 11th, about 1,600 buildings had collapsed or been damaged. At one hospital in Cali, the upper floors with the children’s ward collapsed, and about 600 patients were treated in streets covered with debris. A tower of the Manizales cathedral also fell.

  De la Espriella, who took office on the 7th, declared a national state of emergency three days later and sent the military and police to help with rescue. Cali and Pereira put curfews in place. Aid from neighboring countries began arriving on the 12th.
- **Why it matters**: It was the strongest earthquake in Colombia this century, and buildings collapsed in large cities, including Cali, a city of 2.2 million people. There is a gap of more than 3,600 between the official number of missing people and the citizens’ count, so the full scale of the damage is not yet confirmed. The new government faced a major disaster response three days after taking office.

### 4. Low Danube shuts down Romania’s nuclear plant

- **Date**: Aug 13 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On August 13, Romania’s state nuclear company Nuclearelectrica began a planned shutdown of Unit 2 at the Cernavodă nuclear power plant. Unit 1 had already stopped in late July. The two reactors are cooled with water from the Danube River, and the river level fell to a record low, below what is needed to run them. When running normally, the plant produces about 20% of Romania’s electricity.

  On July 31, Romania declared an alert in the energy sector and spent more than €2 million trying to turn the river’s flow toward the plant. The military broke up large rocks on the riverbed with explosives and sank four barges loaded with stones, but this did not stop the water from falling. The energy ministry said wind power and imports could meet electricity demand, but as a last resort it could limit evening use by large industrial users. The plant’s director said it was not expected to restart within 10 days.

  Hungary’s only nuclear plant, Paks, is also cooled with Danube water and has been partly shut down since late July. The Hungarian government decided to build an underwater dam near the plant.
- **Why it matters**: According to analysis by the European climate service Copernicus, the flow on about two-thirds of the Danube is the lowest in more than 30 years. A drought has stopped a power plant that makes one-fifth of a country’s electricity. The Paks plant is also at risk of stopping completely for the first time in its 44-year history.

## AI

### 1. Anthropic to watermark text written by Claude

- **Date**: Aug 10–14 (Mon–Fri)
- **What happened**: On August 10, Anthropic updated a customer support document to say it would put identifying marks in text and files made by Claude. The transparency rules of the European Union’s AI Act, which took effect on August 2, require AI companies to mark content they generate or edit so that other systems can identify it. Anthropic will apply the marks everywhere Claude is offered, not only in the European Union. This covers its own products, including the API, the Claude apps and Claude Code, as well as Claude services from AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft. Files will carry source metadata under the C2PA standard.

  In a post on the 14th, Anthropic explained how it works. When the model chooses between words with almost the same meaning, it makes the choice with a secret key instead of at random, leaving a pattern across the whole text. It uses the SynthID Text method that Google DeepMind released in 2024, and Anthropic plans to release an API for checking the watermark. Because no characters are added, costs do not rise, and the watermark contains no information that identifies users. Light editing does not fully remove it, but rewriting every word does. Code has fewer word choices, so less of the watermark remains.
- **Why it matters**: European Union rules that took effect on August 2 made machine-readable marks on AI-generated text mandatory. Google, Meta, Microsoft and OpenAI have also signed the same code of practice. Anthropic said that a mark is not conclusive proof that Claude wrote something, and that the lack of one does not guarantee AI did not write it. Some users objected, and dozens on X said they had canceled their subscriptions.

### 2. Meta releases open-weight model Muse Glimmer

- **Date**: Aug 10 (Mon)
- **What happened**: On August 10, Meta released Muse Glimmer, a model with 30 billion parameters, and made its weights available under the Apache 2.0 license. Developers can download it, change it and use it commercially. It was distilled by training on the outputs of Muse Spark, Meta’s most powerful closed model. It runs on a Mac or a PC with a single consumer GPU, and it is built to do agent tasks such as calling tools, writing code and handling files and screenshots without an internet connection. It works with text and images and was trained in more than 100 languages.

  With 4-bit quantization, the weights shrink from about 60 GB to under 16 GB, small enough for a consumer graphics card with 24 GB of memory. The benchmarks Meta released compared it with models of similar size, Google’s Gemma 4 31B and Alibaba’s Qwen 3.6-27B. In a letter the same day, CEO Mark Zuckerberg wrote that Meta would share superintelligence widely so that everyone can use it for free or at a low cost.
- **Why it matters**: Meta has released weights again after stopping for more than a year. The stronger Muse Spark stayed closed, but Alexandr Wang, Meta’s head of superintelligence, said Meta would soon release an open-weight version of Muse Spark 1.2. Meta led open-weight models with Llama from 2023, but stopped releasing weights after Llama 4 did poorly. In the meantime, Chinese models such as Moonshot AI’s Kimi K3, Alibaba’s Qwen and DeepSeek took over the open-weight field.
