## Korea

### 1. Korea knocked out in World Cup group stage

- **Date**: Jun 24–28 (Wed–Sun)
- **What happened**: On June 24 (local time), Korea lost 0-1 to South Africa in its third Group A match at Estadio Monterrey in Guadalupe, Mexico. A draw would have been enough to finish second in the group and reach the round of 32. Head coach Hong Myung-bo put Son Heung-min, who had not scored in the first two games, on the bench for the first time. Korea was pushed back from the first half before conceding the winning goal in the 63rd minute. South Africa is ranked 60th by FIFA, 36 places below Korea (24th).

  With one win and two losses, Korea finished third in Group A with three points, behind Mexico (nine points) and South Africa (four points). From this tournament, the best 8 of the 12 third-place teams also reach the round of 32. On the 28th, the Democratic Republic of the Congo beat Uzbekistan 3-1 in Group K, so Korea could no longer finish in the top 8 and its exit was confirmed. It is the second time Hong has been knocked out in the group stage, after the 2014 World Cup in Brazil.

  On the 28th, President Lee Jae-myung wrote on social media, “It has been proven once again that personnel is everything.” He added, “If you value whose side someone is on more than their ability and choose an incompetent person as commander, the result is plain to see.” The post was aimed at how the national team coach was chosen, and he said he would quickly push ahead with reform of sports administration.
- **Why it matters**: In the first tournament with 48 teams and a knockout stage starting from the round of 32, Korea did not reach the knockout stage. With the president calling the choice of national team coach a personnel failure, the team’s results became part of the government’s plan to reform sports administration.

### 2. KOSPI plunges 9.99%, wiping out 742 trillion won

- **Date**: Jun 23 (Tue)
- **What happened**: On June 23, the KOSPI fell 910.71 points (9.99%) to close at 8,203.84. It came one day after the index hit a record high above 9,000, and it was the largest drop ever in points. At 2:33 p.m., a level-1 circuit breaker stopped trading on the main market for 20 minutes, the fourth time this year. The KOSDAQ also fell 7.94% to 891.52, dropping below 900, and sell-side sidecars, short trading pauses, were triggered on both markets.

  Samsung Electronics closed down 12.31% at 310,000 won and SK hynix down 12.47% at 2,555,000 won. For both stocks, it was the largest one-day drop in more than 17 years, since the 2008 financial crisis. SK hynix had become the largest company on the KOSPI by market value for the first time the day before, and it plunged after hitting 3 million won for the first time ever in pre-market trading that day. The KOSPI’s market value fell by 742.7573 trillion won in one day.

  Foreigners and institutions were each net sellers of more than 4 trillion won, and individuals bought 8.59 trillion won, their largest net purchase ever. To bring its share of Korean stocks to its 20.8% target by the end of this month, the National Pension Service must sell up to 60 trillion won. The won-dollar exchange rate closed at 1,539.1 won.
- **Why it matters**: Investors in single-stock leveraged products on Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, launched on May 27 and drawing about 10 trillion won, lost about 25% in one day. Just as the two chip stocks drove the index up, the fall also started with them, and 514 trillion won of market value disappeared from those two stocks alone.

### 3. Ex-justice minister Park Sung-jae gets 25 years in prison

- **Date**: Jun 22 (Mon)
- **What happened**: On June 22, the Seoul Central District Court sentenced former Justice Minister Park Sung-jae to 25 years in prison on charges of taking an important role in an insurrection and abuse of power. The ruling came from Criminal Division 33 (presiding judge Lee Jin-kwan). Citing the risk of destroying evidence, the court had him taken into custody. The sentence is heavier than the 20 years the special counsel for the insurrection case asked for.

  The court accepted all the charges. Right after martial law was declared on December 3, Park had a team in charge of travel bans stand by. He also had prison capacity checked to hold people who broke the martial law decree, and had prosecutors consider being sent to the joint investigation headquarters of the martial law command. It also found that having justice ministry officials write documents justifying martial law after it failed was abuse of power.

  The court described the December 3 insurrection as “an ‘insurrection from above’ by a president elected by the people and his followers.” About Park, the court said, “He turned away from his duties and took part, thinking martial law might succeed.”
- **Why it matters**: The court found that the justice ministry’s preparations for travel bans and detention during martial law were a key condition for suppressing former President Yoon Suk Yeol’s political opponents and making the National Assembly’s power to lift martial law useless. It set a sentence higher than the special counsel asked for because of the justice minister’s duty to protect the Constitution.
- **Sources**: [Chosun Ilbo](https://n.news.naver.com/article/023/0003983347) / [MBC](https://n.news.naver.com/article/214/0001506945)

### 4. Kim Keon-hee gets seven years for selling influence

- **Date**: Jun 26 (Fri)
- **What happened**: On June 26, the Seoul Central District Court sentenced Kim Keon-hee, wife of former President Yoon Suk Yeol, to seven years in prison. The ruling came from Criminal Division 21 (presiding judge Cho Soon-pyo), and the charges included accepting bribes to use her influence under the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes. The special counsel for Kim Keon-hee had asked for seven years and six months. The court seized items including a painting by Lee Ufan and ordered her to pay 64.8 million won.

  The court found that all the gifts, worth about 300 million won, that Kim received with requests for favors after Yoon was elected were given in return for something. They include jewelry such as a Van Cleef & Arpels necklace from Seohee Construction Chairman Lee Bong-kwan and a gold turtle from former National Education Commission Chair Lee Bae-yong. They also include a Lee Ufan painting worth about 140 million won from former prosecutor Kim Sang-min, and a Dior bag from pastor Choi Jae-young. The court also cited as evidence that Kim understood the exchange the fact that, less than a month after receiving the necklace, Kim received a brooch and asked, “Is there anything I can help you with?”

  The court said, “She received, without hesitation, expensive items that ordinary people could hardly get even once in their lives.” It found that a fake necklace of the same model hidden at the home of the mother-in-law of Kim’s older brother after the investigation began was an attempt to hide traces of the crime.
- **Why it matters**: The court said that if Kim had been a public official, bribery under the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes could have carried up to life in prison. Because bribery charges cannot apply to the president’s spouse, who is a private citizen, Kim was punished for accepting money to use influence, and the court found this the most serious such case. Kim had already been sentenced to four years in prison at the second trial in the Deutsch Motors stock manipulation and Unification Church money case.

## World

### 1. Magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 earthquakes hit Venezuela

- **Date**: Jun 24–28 (Wed–Sun)
- **What happened**: On the evening of June 24, earthquakes of magnitude 7.2 and 7.5 struck northern Venezuela within one minute of each other, bringing down hundreds of buildings. It is the worst earthquake to hit the country in more than 100 years. La Guaira State, on the Caribbean coast north of the capital, Caracas, was hit hardest. The government declared a national emergency.

  The official death toll rose from 188 on the 25th to 920 on the 26th and 1,430 on the 27th, and nearly 3,500 people were injured. Tom Fletcher, the UN humanitarian chief, said more than 50,000 people were missing. Residents dug through rubble with bare hands and shovels, and bodies on motorcycles and pickup trucks arrived one after another at a morgue in Caracas.

  Interim President Delcy Rodríguez said the government would save as many people as possible and that foreign rescue teams were arriving. The main airport in Caracas partly reopened, only for aid flights.
- **Why it matters**: The death toll rose more than sevenfold in two days, and with thousands still under the rubble, it is expected to rise further. Until rescue teams arrived, residents had to search on their own, and criticism of the government’s slow response grew among families of the missing.

### 3. European heat wave breaks national records

- **Date**: Jun 23–28 (Tue–Sun)
- **What happened**: On June 23, France had its hottest day since records began in 1947. It was 44.3°C in Pissos in the Landes department and 42.1°C in Bordeaux, and red heat alerts were issued for 54 departments. The Eiffel Tower and the Louvre cut their opening hours, and Paris banned drinking in public places and canceled its Pride march.

  The heat wave moved east, breaking records as it went. Germany recorded its highest temperature ever for the third day in a row, with 41.7°C on the 28th, and the Czech Republic (41.1°C) and Poland (40.5°C) each set new national records. Denmark also had its hottest day since records began in 1874, with 36.6°C on the 27th. Britain and Switzerland broke their June records.

  World Health Organization (WHO) Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said there had been more than 1,300 excess deaths linked to the heat in Europe since June 21. France’s health ministry counted about 1,000 more deaths than usual since the 24th. In France, at least 74 people drowned in rivers and lakes during the heat wave.
- **Why it matters**: Scientists found that this heat wave would have been almost impossible without the human-caused climate crisis. Tedros said Europe is warming twice as fast as the world average and called on countries to put heat action plans into effect.

### 4. UK Prime Minister Starmer announces resignation

### 5. Israel and Lebanon sign 14-point framework deal

- **Date**: Jun 26–27 (Fri–Sat)
- **What happened**: On June 26, in Washington, Israel and Lebanon signed a 14-point framework agreement to end the fighting between Israel and Hezbollah. Israel agreed to withdraw some of its troops stationed in southern Lebanon.

### 1. OpenAI gives GPT-5.6 only to government-approved firms

- **Date**: Jun 25–26 (Thu–Fri)
- **What happened**: On June 26, OpenAI released a new family of models, GPT-5.6, as a limited preview. It has three models: the flagship Sol, Terra for large-volume work, and the fast, low-cost Luna. At the U.S. government’s request, it is offered only to a small number of partners whose participation has been shared with the government, and the government approves access customer by customer. CEO Sam Altman had told employees about this approach earlier at an internal Q&A session.

  OpenAI said Sol is its strongest model for coding, biology, cybersecurity and long agent tasks. Its price is $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output tokens, and OpenAI added a max mode for deeper reasoning and an ultra mode that runs sub-agents together. Much of the announcement was about safety measures. OpenAI explained that it spent about 700,000 A100e GPU hours on automated red-team testing and trained the model to refuse banned help with cyberattacks even when people try to get around the rules.

  OpenAI said this government access process should not become the long-term default, because it takes the best tools away from users, developers, businesses and cyber defenders. It said it would release the models to the public within weeks.
- **Why it matters**: The U.S. government directly decided which organizations could use a new model. Trump’s executive order in early June had asked AI companies to voluntarily submit their newest models for government review up to 30 days before release. OpenAI said it was building, with the government, a framework under the cybersecurity executive order and a process to repeat each time it releases a model.

### 3. OpenAI unveils its first in-house inference chip, Jalapeño

- **Date**: Jun 24 (Wed)
- **What happened**: On June 24, OpenAI unveiled Jalapeño, its first AI chip of its own, designed and built with Broadcom. It is an application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) only for inference, running already-built models to handle ChatGPT answers and agents such as Codex, not for training. OpenAI’s own models were also used to develop the chip.

  OpenAI said it is still measuring final performance, but that early tests show performance per unit of power well ahead of the best products available today. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan said its performance matches NVIDIA’s Blackwell and Google’s TPU. OpenAI described Jalapeño as the first step in a computing platform spanning several generations, and plans to deploy it by the end of 2026.
- **Why it matters**: The chip came nine months after OpenAI announced its partnership with Broadcom in October 2025, and it aims to reduce reliance on NVIDIA GPUs, which are in short supply. Google, Amazon, Microsoft and Meta are also making their own AI chips. OpenAI said that after models and products, it would directly optimize its whole infrastructure, from chip design to deployment systems.

### 5. Anthropic launches Claude Tag, an AI coworker in Slack

- **Date**: Jun 23 (Tue)
- **What happened**: On June 23, Anthropic released Claude Tag in beta for Claude Enterprise and Team customers. It stays in Slack and works like a team member. When someone tags @Claude in a channel to ask a question or give it a task, Claude breaks the work into steps, handles it with the tools it can use, and posts the results in the thread. It can write and merge pull requests, find revenue figures and analyze data, among other things.

  Everyone in a channel shares one Claude, so anyone can pick up work Claude was doing. Claude follows the channel’s conversations and builds up work context in its memory, and with permission it also gathers information from other channels. Administrators decide which tools, information and channels Claude can use, and separate scopes so that, for example, the memory of a legal team’s Claude does not carry over to an engineering channel. There is also a mode in which it joins conversations on its own to follow up on forgotten tasks.
- **Why it matters**: Earlier Slack integrations answered only when called, but Claude Tag stays in the channel and takes care of work first, using the memory it has built up. Microsoft, Snowflake, Databricks and Glean are also releasing products that give AI agents a company’s work context.
- **Sources**: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/23/anthropics-claude-tag-is-learning-your-company-one-slack-message-at-a-time/) / [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/954921/anthropic-introduced-claude-tag-a-new-ai-agent-slack-integration)
