## Korea

### 1. Democratic Party moves to investigate the chief justice

- **Date**: Oct 27 (Tue)
- **What happened**: On October 27, the Democratic Party decided at a meeting of its lawmakers to hold a parliamentary investigation into Chief Justice Jo Hee-de. It would be the first parliamentary investigation of the head of the judiciary. The party pointed to his refusal to renominate Supreme Court justice candidates, improper use of his expense budget and his refusal to take the witness oath at a parliamentary audit. It plans to report the request for the investigation to the National Assembly plenary session on November 5 and pass the investigation plan around the 12th. Floor leader Han Byung-do said, “It is the National Assembly’s proper duty to fully uncover the allegations and resolve the public’s doubts.”

  Hard-liners in the party called for impeachment, but the leadership chose an investigation first. Floor spokesperson Jeon Eun-soo said the party must also consider whether an impeachment would be upheld by the Constitutional Court, adding, “We should run an investigation and bring out what is actually there.” People Power Party leader Jang Dong-hyuk objected, saying, “An investigation of the chief justice is the build-up to impeaching the chief justice.” The Rebuilding Korea Party called for pursuing the investigation and impeachment together.
- **Why it matters**: If the investigation goes ahead, it will be the first time the legislature directly investigates the head of the judiciary. The investigation plan passes with a majority of members present and a majority of those voting, so the Democratic Party, which holds a majority, can pass it even if the People Power Party objects. The party is also expected to examine why cases linked to political broker Myung Tae-kyun have ended in acquittals one after another, including the appeals court acquittal of Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon in the case over payments for opinion polls.

### 2. Green belt lifted near Seoul for 76,700 homes

- **Date**: Oct 29 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On October 29, the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport announced six candidate sites for new housing land in Gyeonggi Province, within 5 km of the Seoul border. The government will lift green belt (development restriction zone) rules there and build 76,700 public housing units. The sites are Gimpo Ara (37,000 units), Guri Geonwon (14,600), Hanam Misa K-Culture (12,000), Hanam Gamil 2 (6,500), Goyang Daegok Knowledge Convergence District (4,000) and Uijeongbu Darakwon (2,600). Gimpo Ara is more than twice the size of Incheon’s Gyeyang district (18,000 units), part of the third round of new towns. The government said it would cut the administrative process, which used to take 68 months from site announcement to construction, and start construction by 2030.

  Seoul was left out. Sites that had been mentioned, such as the returned land of the U.S. military base in Yongsan and the reserve forces training ground in Naegok-dong, Seocho District, were not included. Land Minister Hong Ji-sun said, “We announced first the sites that had been agreed with local governments.” Gimpo Mayor Lee Gi-hyung held a press conference the same day and objected: “One-sided supply without thorough transport and self-sufficiency measures will turn Gimpo into a consumer bedroom town.”
- **Why it matters**: The locations of all 103,900 units of new housing land in the capital area, promised in the August 13 “rapid housing supply plan,” are now set. The government said it chose transport hubs so that people who need homes, such as young people and newly married couples, can move in. It also decided to lift the land transaction permit zone on Seoul’s green belt around November 3, except for part of Songpa District. Some read this as a step back from building homes on Seoul’s green belt.

### 3. Surprise 1% third-quarter growth as consumer spending falls

- **Date**: Oct 27 (Tue)
- **What happened**: The Bank of Korea’s advance estimate, released on October 27, showed real gross domestic product (GDP) grew 1.0% in the third quarter from the previous quarter and 3.4% from a year earlier. That is 0.7 percentage points above the bank’s August forecast of 0.3%. The bank said annual growth will reach 3.6% even with no growth in the fourth quarter, and 4% is possible if the fourth quarter grows 1.4% or more. The Ministry of Economy and Finance said per capita gross national income (GNI) is certain to pass $40,000 this year, 12 years after it first passed $30,000.

  Semiconductors drove the growth. Facility investment rose 4.1%, led by machinery for making chips, and exports rose 2.3%. Lee Dong-won, head of the bank’s Economic Statistics Department 2, estimated that semiconductors made up about half of total growth. As chip export prices rose, real gross domestic income (GDI) grew 18.8% from a year earlier, the highest rate since the fourth quarter of 1977.

  Private consumption fell 0.2%, its first decline in three years and nine months, since the fourth quarter of 2022. Spending fell on both goods such as cars and home appliances and services such as finance and dining.
- **Why it matters**: The growth rate and the economy that households feel have moved far apart. A stock market correction and higher prices from the prolonged Middle East war reduced people’s room to spend, so higher incomes did not turn into more spending. Because half of the growth came from semiconductors, there are concerns that a chip downturn would hit the whole economy hard. The Bank of Korea raised its base rate twice, in July and August, then held it at 3.0% on October 22, and named third-quarter growth as a key indicator for another hike. Markets expect a further 0.25-point rise at the Monetary Policy Board meeting on November 26.

### 4. North Korea fires missiles and shows off suicide drones

- **Date**: Oct 29–Nov 1 (Thu–Sun)
- **What happened**: On October 29, North Korea fired about 10 short-range ballistic missiles and other projectiles from the Wonsan area into the East Sea. They flew about 250 km and are believed to be 600 mm multiple rocket launchers (KN-25 type). It was the North’s 17th ballistic missile launch this year, and came six days after South Korea’s Defense Ministry formally proposed military talks between the two Koreas on the 23rd. North Korea has not answered the proposal.

  On the same day, leader Kim Jong Un visited the exhibition “Unmanned Aviation Technology Development 2026.” North Korea showed more than 30 types of drones, including a stealth unmanned combat aircraft and a cardboard suicide drone. On the 30th, it released images of suicide drones striking models of a South Korean front-line guard post (GP), a K9 self-propelled howitzer and a U.S. M1 Abrams tank. Kim said, “Strength is peace, and peace can be guaranteed only by strength.”

  On November 1, North Korea said it had test-fired a ballistic missile with a new cluster warhead on October 31. The Joint Chiefs of Staff believe the missile flew less than 100 km from Jongju, North Pyongan Province, to a target island in the West Sea. North Korea claimed the warhead covers about 12 hectares, the size of 18 soccer fields. The military assessed it as a KN-24, with a maximum range of 400 km, or a KN-35, with a range of about 120 km.

  On the 29th, residents of border villages such as Daeseong-dong in Paju, Gyeonggi Province, said they heard sounds from the North like its old loudspeaker broadcasts. The Joint Chiefs of Staff and the National Intelligence Service said no loudspeaker operation had been confirmed.
- **Why it matters**: North Korea turned down the offer of talks and instead showed one conventional weapon after another aimed at South Korean military sites. One analyst said the drones on display form strategic (reconnaissance), operational (strike) and tactical (loitering munition) tiers, almost the same as the Russian military’s current drone structure. Military experts see an aim, based on the North’s combat experience in the Ukraine war, to overwhelm South Korean forces on the peninsula without nuclear weapons. A KN-35 deployed near the armistice line could reach U.S. bases in Pyeongtaek and Osan in Gyeonggi Province.

### 5. Hacks hit 20 financial firms, first loss of customer money

- **Date**: Oct 26–29 (Mon–Thu)
- **What happened**: Hacking damage that began at banks spread to insurers, securities firms, asset managers and crypto exchanges. Mirae Asset Life confirmed a leak of personal data early on October 26. It was the first breach reported by an insurer. Daishin Securities said on the 28th that personal and credit data of 38,490 customers had leaked through a server for opening accounts online. At Bithumb, data of 6,444 customers leaked, including resident registration numbers, account numbers and copies of ID cards, and the data of 844 of them was not encrypted. By the 29th, 20 financial firms had been confirmed as victims.

  On the 29th, it emerged that around the 24th, abnormal withdrawals from the hot wallet of crypto exchange Pravang had taken about 100 million won of users’ assets. It is the first case in the series of financial-sector hacks in which customer assets were actually taken. Whether the same attacker was behind the earlier hacks has not been confirmed.

  The Financial Security Institute warned financial firms that 4,404,101 scanning attempts had come from North Korean internet address (IP) ranges in a single day on the 21st. It was the first official confirmation of attacks from North Korean IP addresses since the hacks came to light in early October. Financial authorities said it is hard to conclude that North Korea was behind them, because a third party could have disguised itself with North Korean addresses. The National Security Office held an emergency review meeting on the 28th.
- **Why it matters**: Hacking that had been limited to data leaks has now led to the theft of customer assets. Until now, the leaked data had been names, contact details and loan information, with no data such as account passwords or card numbers that could be used directly for fraudulent payments. The number of people affected by the leak at KB Kookmin Bank grew from the 119 first found to 2,210. Attacks suspected of using artificial intelligence (AI) agents are spreading to public institutions as well.

### 4. FBI says North Korea stole $6.3 billion from Binance

- **Date**: Oct 29–Nov 1 (Thu–Sun)
- **What happened**: Binance, the world’s largest crypto exchange, was hacked on October 29, and about $6.3 billion (about 8.4 trillion won) in crypto assets was taken. It took about 20 minutes, starting at 4:05 p.m. U.S. Eastern Time. Binance said its first findings suggest that a DevOps engineer’s laptop was compromised, letting the attacker get into its internal systems and carry out unauthorized withdrawals. It halted deposits and withdrawals and, with blockchain firms, froze about $3 billion. Stablecoin issuers Tether and Circle also said they froze $1.5 billion and $800 million, respectively.

  On the 31st, the U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) named the North Korean hacking group TraderTraitor as the attacker. The same group took $1.5 billion from the exchange Bybit last year. The FBI said the hackers were converting the stolen assets mainly into ether and asked the industry to block transactions linked to the wallet addresses involved. Trump said that if North Korea is confirmed as the attacker, he would talk to North Korea about it.

  By November 1, Binance had covered nearly all of the customers’ losses with its own assets, and it resumed withdrawals in stages.
- **Why it matters**: It is seen as the largest cyber theft in history, four times the Bybit theft in a single attack. South Korean financial firms are also facing mass scans from North Korean IP addresses, which adds to the security burden on Korean exchanges and financial firms. Some read Trump’s remark as leaving room to use the hack as an occasion for talks between North Korea and the United States.

### 2. OpenAI and Anthropic say AI refuted a key computing theory

- **Date**: Oct 26–27 (Mon–Tue)
- **What happened**: On October 26, OpenAI announced AI solutions to three hard problems in mathematics, computer science and physics. The computer science result says the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) is false. The hypothesis says that the time needed to solve a certain kind of logic puzzle grows exponentially as the number of variables grows. OpenAI claimed its AI model found a faster algorithm. Hours later, Anthropic released its own refutation.

  The mathematics result claims to solve the Kakeya conjecture in all dimensions. A proof of the three-dimensional version was among the work honored with a Fields Medal this year. The physics result concerns quantum chromodynamics, which describes the strong nuclear force, and has not been released.

  OpenAI used a different release method for each field. For mathematics, it released the output as it was. For computer science, an outside group of researchers will explain the result. For physics, it left the decision on how to release it to a group of outside experts. The researchers who first proposed the ETH in 1999 and MIT professor Ryan Williams are helping to interpret the result.
- **Why it matters**: If the refutation holds, a widely used assumption about which problems are hard to compute turns out to be false. The ETH is connected to the P versus NP problem, and encryption relies on the fact that checking an answer is easy while finding it is hard. Williams said there are two or three parts of the proof that look alien, so the whole field needs to review it together. Researchers who objected when OpenAI released hundreds of preprints at once in early October still point to the burden of checking the work.
