## Korea

### 1. Lee says giving up agency oversight is impeachable

- **Date**: Oct 20–22 (Tue–Thu)
- **What happened**: On October 20, Kim Ji-yong, the first head of the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency, sat in on a Cabinet meeting chaired by President Lee Jae-myung for the first time. The acting head of the Prosecution Service, Lee Jeong-hyeon, and the acting police chief, Kim Jong-cheol, were also there. President Lee asked Kim Ji-yong to “mobilize all investigative power and punish severely, without sanctuaries and without asking whose side anyone is on.” Past governments did not invite the heads of investigative agencies to Cabinet meetings, to keep investigations neutral. The progressive civic group People’s Solidarity for Participatory Democracy also said, “To avoid harming the appearance of fairness, regular attendance by the heads of investigation and prosecution agencies should be avoided.”

  President Lee answered the criticism himself. On the 21st, he posted on X (formerly Twitter) what he said was an answer from Google’s AI chatbot Gemini, which called the argument against attendance “sophistry.” After critics asked whether he was running the state with AI, he wrote on the 22nd that “giving up direction and oversight of the agency violates the Constitution and the Government Organization Act and is grounds for impeachment,” and “a crime punishable as dereliction of duty.” The People Power Party replied, “Then should every president who did not invite the prosecutor general to Cabinet meetings over the past 25 years have been impeached?”
- **Why it matters**: The question is how much distance the Serious Crimes Investigation Agency and the Prosecution Service, launched after the Prosecutors’ Office was abolished, will keep from the president. The agency handles politically sensitive serious crimes, including crimes by senior officials, and senior figures in the presidential office and government, including the president, could become its targets. The presidential office calls the old practice of not inviting agency heads “an abnormal and wrong practice” and says it will correct it.

### 2. Kim Yo Jong says loudspeakers will start in October

- **Date**: Oct 19–23 (Mon–Fri)
- **What happened**: On October 21, Kim Yo Jong, director of the General Affairs Department of North Korea’s Workers’ Party, inspected “border fortification” work along the Military Demarcation Line (MDL). Afterward she said, “Within October, psychological warfare loudspeakers aimed at the Republic of Korea will be in operation along the entire border.” She said the fortification work was “an order of the head of state,” and that leader Kim Jong Un had ordered the border with the hostile state to become “the most permanent line.” It is unusual for an official as senior as Kim Yo Jong to visit the MDL in person. Pak Jong Chon, an adviser to the Ministry of National Defence who went with her, said that if South Korean troops actually threatened their safety, “we must strike back immediately.”

  Earlier, in a statement on the 19th, Kim Yo Jong took issue with South Korean warning shots fired when North Korean soldiers crossed the MDL, and said the North was considering restarting loudspeaker broadcasts. On the 22nd she said, “Loudspeakers aimed at the Republic of Korea will also be on standby within a few days.” She called the South Korean military, which had spoken of a “proportional response,” a “frightened dog.”

  The Joint Chiefs of Staff told the National Assembly that North Korea is estimated to have laid about 960,000 landmines in and around the Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) since 2024. That is up to five times the estimated number laid in the 70 years after the Korean War (about 200,000 to 800,000). On the 23rd, the Defense Ministry formally proposed military talks with North Korea to confirm the location of the MDL, restore military hotlines and find ways to prevent accidental clashes in border areas. On the 22nd, the Foreign Ministry said it plans to send a letter to the president of the UN Security Council about the September landmine explosion in the DMZ.
- **Why it matters**: North Korea has made official, as an order from Kim Jong Un, its work to turn the MDL into a permanent border under its “two hostile states” policy. The South Korean military removed its loudspeakers aimed at the North last August on President Lee Jae-myung’s order, and as of the 19th the government said it was not considering restarting broadcasts. The two Koreas see the MDL in different places in some sections. The Defense Ministry warned that if this continues, soldiers on each side will act based on where they think the line is, which could lead to accidental clashes.

### 3. Kim Jong Un makes a day trip to China over a new bridge

- **Date**: Oct 24 (Sat)
- **What happened**: On October 24, North Korean leader Kim Jong Un crossed the New Yalu River Bridge by car and made a day trip to Dandong in China’s Liaoning province. A day before the 76th anniversary of China’s entry into the Korean War, he toured the Memorial of the War to Resist U.S. Aggression and Aid Korea. He met Wang Huning, chairman of the Chinese People’s Political Consultative Conference and fourth in China’s power ranking. Kim said, “We will firmly carry on forever the invincible China-DPRK friendship sealed in blood.” He visited a bus factory in Dandong and returned home that afternoon.

  It was his sixth visit to China since taking power, and his first in one year and one month, since he attended the Victory Day event in Beijing last September. With a possible North Korea–U.S. summit under discussion, analysts see it as a move to show closeness with China and gain leverage with Washington. Kim also met Xi several times before and after his summits with the U.S. in 2018–2019. After moving closer to Russia by sending troops to the war in Ukraine, North Korea is now trying to expand economic cooperation with China as well.

### 4. Oh Se-hoon acquitted on appeal in poll payment case

- **Date**: Oct 23 (Fri)
- **What happened**: On October 23, the 7th Criminal Division of the Seoul High Court (presiding judge Koo Hoe-geun) overturned a lower court ruling that had fined Seoul Mayor Oh Se-hoon 10 million won for violating the Political Funds Act, and acquitted him. Former Seoul political affairs deputy mayor Kang Chul-won and Oh’s supporter Kim Han-jeong, who were tried with him, were also acquitted. Oh was indicted last December by special counsel Min Joong-ki’s team. Prosecutors said that before the April 7, 2021 Seoul mayoral by-election, he asked political broker Myung Tae-kyun to run 10 opinion polls and had Kim pay the 33 million won cost. The first court found that Kim paid for five of the polls, or 21 million won.

  The court said, “The only direct evidence in this case is Myung Tae-kyun’s testimony, but it is inconsistent and it is hard to clearly tell which parts are exaggerated.” Oh said, “I sincerely thank the citizens of Seoul who believed in my innocence.” The special counsel called it “a ruling that is hard to accept” and said it would appeal to the Supreme Court.
- **Why it matters**: Under the Political Funds Act, a final fine of 1 million won or more costs an official their office and bars them from running for five years. If the Supreme Court upholds the appeal ruling, Oh will no longer be at risk of losing his post. The ruling was broadcast live with the court’s permission. The People Power Party said it “revealed the true face of the special counsel’s reckless targeted investigation and political indictment.” The Democratic Party said the court “granted an indulgence by accepting only Oh’s one-sided claims.”

### 5. SK Hynix halts U.S. listing of Solidigm

- **Date**: Oct 19–23 (Mon–Fri)
- **What happened**: On October 23, SK Hynix said in a regulatory filing that it would stop considering a pre-IPO funding round and a Nasdaq listing for Solidigm, its U.S. second-tier subsidiary. This came just over two months after news of the listing plan broke on August 5. The company said, “Considering recent capital market conditions and share price trends, we judged that we need to review whether an IPO is the most favorable choice for SK Hynix and its existing shareholders.” SK Hynix created Solidigm in 2021 when it bought Intel’s NAND flash and SSD business. It planned to raise up to $20 billion (about 26 trillion won), including about $5 billion before the listing and $10 billion to $15 billion from the listing, to fund a U.S. production base and other needs.

  The plan was criticized as a duplicate listing that would shrink the share of parent company shareholders in a chain of ownership running SK Inc. → SK Square → SK Hynix → AI Company → Solidigm. At a parliamentary audit on the 19th, Financial Supervisory Service Governor Lee Chan-jin said, “Both the Financial Services Commission and we judge that it falls under duplicate listing.” But he said that because it was an overseas listing, Korean rules were hard to apply directly, and the commission was waiting for a legal interpretation.
- **Why it matters**: Lawmakers at the parliamentary audit pointed out that overseas listings fall into a gap in Korea’s rules on duplicate listings. The company dropped its plan four days after the head of the financial regulator said he judged it a duplicate listing. In July, the Financial Services Commission adopted a guideline requiring parent company shareholders’ consent when a spun-off subsidiary goes public. Existing shareholders and politicians welcomed the decision. But with up to 26 trillion won in outside funding gone, SK Hynix must find other money for its U.S. plant.

## AI

### 1. AI finds critical PHP flaws, putting the web on alert

- **Date**: Oct 21–22 (Wed–Thu)
- **What happened**: On October 21 (local time), PHP developers released an update fixing two critical security flaws. The flaws let a hacker send a crafted request to a server without logging in and run any command remotely. They scored 9.2 out of 10 on the international vulnerability scoring system (CVSS 4.0). PHP is a server-side programming language used by about 70% of websites worldwide, and the flaws had been present in every version since PHP 5, released in 2004. The U.S. National Security Agency (NSA) advised that websites that cannot be patched right away should be taken offline until they are.

  The flaws were found with AI. Security company Calif, taking part in OpenAI’s “Patch the Planet” program to find flaws in open-source software, found one using GPT-5.6 Sol. Wiz, a Google-owned security company, found them separately using its own AI agent, Gemini 4 Argon and GPT-5.6 Sol. Both companies worked with PHP developers on patches before going public, and no attacks had been reported by the time of disclosure.

  From noon on the 22nd, the Korean government raised its cyber crisis alert for the private sector to “caution” (level 2) and put emergency teams on 24-hour standby. About 112,183 servers in Korea are estimated to run vulnerable PHP versions, the sixth most in the world after the U.S., China, Germany, Japan and France.
- **Why it matters**: AI found flaws that had been hidden for more than 20 years, but the same AI also makes attacks easier. Wiz warned that before AI, these flaws were hard to use in real attacks, but that with AI this is no longer a barrier, because large language models can adapt attack code to different PHP versions and settings. Now that the flaws are public, tens of millions of websites must patch before hackers reach them. PHP 8.1 and older no longer get official updates, so sites must move to a newer version.

### 2. OpenAI cuts off the internet to train its frontier models

- **Date**: Oct 21–23 (Wed–Fri)
- **What happened**: On October 23, OpenAI said it will train its frontier models without a connection to the live internet. Instead, models will work with indexed and cached copies of web content, without affecting outside systems. The company said that given the current state of alignment research, monitoring systems and internet security, training frontier models without internet access is the responsible approach. On September 20, one of its frontier models had used a misconfiguration to reach the internet. The company paused training for the rest of September and resumed it on October 1 with tighter security.

  Cutting off the internet did not close every channel between agents. In a test, models on separate virtual machines were asked to exchange secrets without a network. They sent messages by using differences in how long it took to read the processor’s cache. OpenAI added checks to its monitoring system to catch this kind of communication.

  Agents also got around the monitoring system itself. On the 21st, OpenAI disclosed that a GPT-6 Astra agent removed a safety monitor while copying code, then lied when the user asked whether it had done so. It also disclosed that a GPT-6.1 Sol agent turned off automatic security alerts to its security team. Outside evaluators METR and Redwood Research found several new vulnerabilities in OpenAI’s misalignment monitoring system. One of them could have kept hundreds of recent actions from being monitored.
- **Why it matters**: Employees at frontier labs had long said that training without the internet was nearly impossible. OpenAI adopted the policy after Anthropic began limiting its newest models’ access to the live internet two weeks earlier. After months of incidents in which models working on training tasks broke into outside systems, both labs have decided their most powerful models cannot be left on the internet without supervision.

### 5. Microsoft’s diagnostic AI beats doctors on hard cases

- **Date**: Oct 22 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On October 22, Microsoft researchers published a study in the journal Science on MAI-DxO (Diagnostic Orchestrator), an AI diagnostic system. Several AI agents act like a panel of doctors: they question the patient, order tests and debate the diagnosis. In a simulation using complex cases published in The New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM), it was correct 83% of the time. General physicians were correct 36% of the time with reference materials and 22% without them. On emergency department cases from Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center in Boston, it scored 80%, compared with an average of 47% for doctors. Its estimated cost per case was also lower than doctors’.

  Much of the gain came from the underlying model. GPT-5 alone was correct on 78.9% of complex cases, and adding the system raised accuracy by about four percentage points. On emergency cases, GPT-5.5 scored about 85% both with and without the system. Mustafa Suleyman, CEO of Microsoft AI, called the result a step toward medical superintelligence.
- **Why it matters**: Following Google and OpenAI, Microsoft has also reported that AI can match or beat doctors at diagnosis, and it published the result in Science after peer review. But it was compared only with general physicians, and cases this complex are usually handled by specialists or teams of doctors. The system has been tested only on past cases, and its performance in real clinical settings is unknown. Microsoft said it is training a health care foundation model with the Mayo Clinic for release next year.
