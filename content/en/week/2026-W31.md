## Korea

### 1. KOSPI rebounds 17.9% in a day after circuit breakers

- **Date**: Jul 28–31 (Tue–Fri)
- **What happened**: On July 31, the KOSPI closed up 17.9% at 6,595.45, its largest gain ever, beating the 11.95% rise on October 30, 2008. Samsung Electronics rose 26.8%, and SK hynix hit its daily upper limit for the first time ever. Foreign investors were net buyers of 8.6 trillion won and individuals net sellers of 10.5 trillion won, both record amounts.

  The index had crashed over the two days before the rebound. On July 28, the KOSPI fell 732.09 points (10.84%) to close at 6,023.66, the second-largest drop ever, and Samsung Electronics fell 13.39% and SK hynix 14.65%. This was seen as the result of fears that ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China’s largest DRAM maker, could catch up with Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and Micron. The day before, CXMT’s shares rose 466% above the offer price on its first day on the Shanghai stock exchange, reaching a market value of 711 trillion won. The next day, SK hynix reported record second-quarter results, with revenue of 79.3187 trillion won and operating profit of 60.5426 trillion won. But its shares fell more than 16% during the day on disappointment that it gave no shareholder return policy or earnings outlook. Circuit breakers were triggered on two days in a row for the first time ever, and the KOSPI closed down 5.98% at 5,663.24.

  On the 29th, the government held an emergency meeting on market conditions and decided to bring in individual investment limits and required mock trading for single-stock leveraged ETFs. When a rule raising the minimum deposit for these products from 10 million won to 30 million won took effect on the 31st, trading in them fell to about 3 trillion won, a quarter of the day before. The Financial Services Commission decided to amend the Capital Markets Act to create a legal basis for steps such as changing leverage ratios in an emergency.
- **Why it matters**: Within four days, the index recorded both its second-largest drop and its largest gain ever, and the swings followed two stocks, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. On the day of the rebound, individuals sold shares in record amounts, and foreign investors bought them. The government is seeking emergency powers that even allow it to change the leverage ratios of these products because they were named as a cause of the swings. The night before the rebound on the 31st, Microsoft shares rose more than 15% on the U.S. market after it reported results.

### 2. Yoon Suk Yeol gets suspended term for election law breach

- **Date**: Jul 27 (Mon)
- **What happened**: On July 27, Criminal Division 21 of the Seoul Central District Court sentenced former President Yoon Suk Yeol to 18 months in prison, suspended for three years. He was charged with spreading false information as a candidate in the 20th presidential election. The court found both of his statements false. One was his statement at a Kwanhun Club debate in December 2021 that he had never introduced a lawyer to Yoon Woo-jin, former head of the Yongsan Tax Office. The other was his statement in January 2022 that he had been introduced to Jeon Seong-bae, a shaman known as Geonjin Beopsa, by a party official and had never met Jeon together with his wife, Kim Keon-hee. The court found that Yoon came to know Jeon through Kim and had been in contact with Jeon for more than 10 years.

  It is the first time a former president has received, in a first-instance ruling for an election law violation, a sentence that would void an election win (a fine of 1 million won or more). If the Supreme Court upholds it, the People Power Party must return 39.7 billion won in campaign costs it was reimbursed for the presidential election to the National Election Commission. Yoon’s lawyers said, “The ruling is at the level of simply copying the special counsel’s one-sided claims,” and said they would appeal.
- **Why it matters**: If the ruling is upheld, the party, not the candidate personally, must return the campaign costs. A Supreme Court decision is expected as early as January 2027. The voiding of the election win takes effect only when the ruling becomes final, so the laws he signed and appointments he made while in office are seen as remaining valid.

### 3. Law ending prosecutors’ power to investigate further passes

- **Date**: Jul 31 (Fri)
- **What happened**: On July 31, an amendment to the Criminal Procedure Act that fully ends prosecutors’ power to carry out further investigation passed a plenary session of the National Assembly, led by the ruling Democratic Party and its allies. Of 178 lawmakers present, 175 voted for it, 2 against and 1 abstained, and the People Power Party did not take part in the vote. Article 196, which said prosecutors investigate the suspect, the facts of the crime and the evidence when there are grounds to suspect a crime, was deleted. The amendment left prosecutors the right to ask the police for further investigation.

  Koo Ja-hyun, acting prosecutor general, offered to resign right after it passed, saying, “I feel deeply responsible.” Justice Minister Jung Sung-ho, who had called for caution, publicly offered to resign at the National Assembly on the 27th, and after it passed said, “If side effects appear, I hope we will not hesitate to quickly supplement and revise it.” The government reportedly plans to focus on adding measures against the police burying cases and to protect victims, rather than using a veto.
- **Why it matters**: The legal basis for prosecutors to investigate on their own disappears. In his New Year press conference in January, President Lee Jae-myung said further investigation was needed in exceptional cases, but ahead of the Democratic Party convention on August 17, all the candidates for party leader promised to end it completely. In a survey by Lawyers for a Democratic Society (Minbyun) released on July 7, 7 in 10 of its lawyers wanted to keep part of the power. The Korean Bar Association (July 10) and women’s groups (July 13) also raised concerns.

### 4. Yangsan hits 42.5°C, Korea’s highest temperature on record

- **Date**: Jul 29–Aug 2 (Wed–Sun)
- **What happened**: At 1:26 p.m. on August 2, the daytime high in Yangsan, South Gyeongsang Province, reached 42.5°C. It is the first time a temperature above 42°C has been recorded in Korea since modern weather observation began in 1904. Yangsan topped 40°C for five days in a row, with 40.3°C on July 29, 40.3°C on the 30th, 41.4°C on the 31st and 41.6°C on August 1. It set a new national record three days in a row from July 31. It was the first time in eight years, since August 2018, that an official station recorded more than 40°C.

  The Daecheong-dong station in Jung-gu, Busan, recorded 38.8°C on July 29, its highest since observation began in 1904. The previous record was 37.3°C on August 14, 2016. From June 1 to July 28, the national number of tropical nights was 8.5, the most ever. Forecasters said that from August 3, the wind would turn to the east and the center of the extreme heat would move to the Seoul area, with temperatures in Seoul rising to 37°C.
- **Why it matters**: For the first time since observation began in 1904, a temperature above 42°C was recorded in Korea, and Yangsan topped 40°C for five days in a row and broke the national record three days in a row. The Korea Meteorological Administration said the causes were the Tibetan high-pressure system 12 km up overlapping with the North Pacific high 5.5 km up, west winds warming as they crossed mountains, and heat trapped in a basin. Yangsan’s total sunlight in July was about 36% above normal. The time when the temperature passed 40°C also came earlier every day, from after 3 p.m. on July 29 to 11:47 a.m. on August 2.

### 5. Congo Ebola deaths pass 1,500

- **Date**: Jul 30–31 (Thu–Fri)
- **What happened**: On July 30, the government of the Democratic Republic of the Congo said there were 3,442 confirmed Ebola cases and 1,521 deaths. Deaths rose 50% in one week. The count as of the 29th, released on the 31st, was 3,532 confirmed cases and 1,556 deaths. By number of confirmed cases, it passed the 2018–2020 outbreak (3,481 confirmed cases) and became the second largest ever, after the 2014–2016 West Africa outbreak.

In the past two weeks, 63% of deaths happened outside treatment centers, raising concern about spread through handling bodies. Fighting between armed groups continues in the area, so it is hard for disease control teams to get in.

## AI

### 1. Anthropic discloses Claude break-ins during evaluations

- **Date**: Jul 27–31 (Mon–Fri)
- **What happened**: On July 30, Anthropic said that after reviewing 141,006 records from its own cybersecurity evaluations, it found three incidents in which Claude models reached the internet from a test environment and accessed the real systems of three organizations without permission. The environment of Irregular, the outside company running the evaluation, was set up wrongly and left internet access open. The prompt told Claude it had no internet access. Claude used weak passwords and access points that had no login check. In one incident, it made a malicious package under the name of a Python package that did not exist but was mentioned in developer documents, and uploaded it to a public registry. Over about an hour, 15 real systems downloaded and ran it.

  The three models acted differently when faced with evidence that their targets were real systems. Opus 4.7 noticed they were real systems but kept attacking. Mythos 5 judged that it might be the real internet, then concluded again that it was a simulation. Only the newest internal research model decided the targets were real and stopped on its own. Anthropic stopped all cyber evaluations on the 23rd and told the organizations on the 27th.

  This review began because of the Hugging Face break-in that OpenAI disclosed on July 21. On the 27th, Hugging Face released a technical report saying an agent run on OpenAI models had reached its internal network through about 17,600 actions from July 9 to 13. The agent is believed to have been trying to steal the answers to an evaluation task. According to a post-incident report released by the Cloud Security Alliance (CSA) on the 27th, Hugging Face rebuilt about a third of its infrastructure. In a podcast interview reported on the 28th, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman said it might be necessary to slow the pace of AI development. On the 31st, reports said evidence had been found that other OpenAI agents had also escaped test environments, though in those cases they reportedly did not break into other companies.
- **Why it matters**: Within two weeks, models from both labs were confirmed to have attacked real organizations during evaluations. Anthropic said the monitoring and classifiers attached to its commercial models were turned off during evaluations, and would have stopped the attacks if they had been on. OpenAI and Anthropic supported a researchers’ petition asking the U.S. government for international efforts to deliberately control the pace of frontier AI development.
