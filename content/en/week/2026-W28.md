## Korea

### 1. Officer arrested for destroying evidence in Jang Yun-gi case

- **Date**: Jul 6–11 (Mon–Sat)
- **What happened**: On July 8, the Gwangju District Court issued an arrest warrant for Inspector A, head of an investigation team in the criminal division of the Gwangsan Police Station in Gwangju. Inspector A is accused of destroying evidence by ordering that cable ties be left in the car of Jang Yun-gi (23) instead of being seized during a search. Jang killed Lee Chae-won, a high school girl in her second year, on May 5 as she was walking home. The cable ties are seen as evidence supporting signs of kidnapping. On the 7th, the Gwangju District Prosecutors’ Office searched the home of Jang’s father, a serving police inspector in the Gwangju area, and found the cable ties.

  Two days after the crime, the Gwangsan station’s investigation team handed Jang’s car over to the father and gave him the door code to Jang’s studio apartment. The team also did not secure as evidence a damaged sex toy found at Jang’s home, and the father threw it away. Police sent Jang’s case to prosecutors on a charge of ordinary murder, but prosecutors found clues of a sexual assault motive through further investigation and changed the charge to murder in the course of rape and other crimes. In questioning by police on the 10th, the father denied trying to destroy evidence, saying, “My son will have to live in prison, so I moved the things in the studio and the car to sort them out.”

  The investigation spread to the leadership. Prosecutors named Kim, a senior superintendent general who was then head of the Gwangsan station, and Park, the superintendent heading its criminal division, as suspects on charges including destroying evidence. This brought the number of police officers named as suspects to four. Several members of the Gwangsan investigation team told a police special investigation team, in effect, that “the station head kept us from applying the charge of murder in the course of rape,” while the station head denied this. On the 11th, the Korean National Police Agency searched the offices of the head of the Gwangju Metropolitan Police Agency and the head of the Gwangsan station, and expanded its special investigation team into a 41-member special investigation unit.
- **Why it matters**: Prosecutors’ further investigation revealed that police had missed evidence supporting a sex crime charge in the early investigation and handed it over to the suspect’s family. The case came up while ending prosecutors’ power to carry out further investigation was being discussed. On the 6th, Hong Seok-gi, head of the National Office of Investigation, said, “We will stake everything on this.”

### 2. Canada picks Germany over Hanwha Ocean for submarines

- **Date**: Jul 6 (Mon)
- **What happened**: On July 6, at a naval base in Halifax, Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney announced that Germany’s ThyssenKrupp Marine Systems (TKMS) had been chosen as the preferred bidder for its next-generation submarine program. Hanwha Ocean, which competed until the end, became the reserve bidder. Canada said it keeps the right to negotiate with Hanwha Ocean if talks with TKMS break down. The program will buy up to 12 non-nuclear submarines to replace four bought from Britain in 1998. Including maintenance and operating costs over 30 years, it is estimated to be worth up to 60 trillion won.

  Carney said Canada had chosen the best platform and partnership to meet both its strategic security and its economic interests, and that the TKMS platform was best suited to Arctic waters and fully interoperable with NATO. The Korean government had campaigned for the contract with the military and industry together, including sending the presidential chief of staff as a special envoy. Hanwha Ocean shares fell 22.65% on the 7th. Hanwha also dropped a plan to set up a shipbuilding skills training center in Ontario.
- **Why it matters**: Hanwha Ocean stressed its technology and operating ability, but Canada was seen as giving more weight to its network of cooperation with NATO allies and to links with Arctic operations. Canada chose a consortium from Germany and Norway, fellow NATO members. On the 8th, it was confirmed that the U.S. Defense Department and Navy had sent Korean shipbuilders requests for information about their capacity to build warships and refueling ships. It is the first time since U.S. President Donald Trump took office and the two countries began talks on MASGA, their shipbuilding cooperation plan, that the U.S. has used a request for information to ask Korean shipyards about warship capacity.

### 5. Yoon’s seven-year term for blocking his arrest made final

- **Date**: Jul 9 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On July 9, the Supreme Court’s 3rd Division upheld a lower court’s sentence of seven years in prison for former President Yoon Suk Yeol. The charges included obstructing the performance of official duties by force and obstructing the exercise of rights by abuse of power. It is the first Supreme Court ruling on Yoon, 583 days after the December 3 martial law. He was found guilty of blocking the first and second attempts by the Corruption Investigation Office for High-ranking Officials (CIO) to carry out arrest warrants. He was also found guilty of violating the right of nine Cabinet members to deliberate on martial law and of writing and then destroying a martial law declaration after the fact. Other charges he was found guilty of were ordering false material to be given to foreign media, and ordering limits on access to records of secure phones. The Supreme Court said a president’s immunity from prosecution does not block investigation, and recognized the CIO’s power to investigate the crime of insurrection. Appeals by both the special counsel and Yoon’s side were rejected.

  At the time of the ruling, Yoon was on trial at the Seoul High Court in his appeal on the charge of leading an insurrection. His lawyers’ request for a recess was granted, and he watched the live broadcast of the ruling on a mobile phone. His lawyers said they would challenge the ruling as unconstitutional through constitutional court procedures, such as a petition against the court ruling. On the same day, the Seoul Central District Court sentenced Park Jong-joon, former head of the Presidential Security Service, to four years and Kim Sung-hoon, its former deputy head, to five years for blocking the arrest warrant. The court had them taken into custody.
- **Why it matters**: Yoon is the fifth former president convicted with a final ruling since Korea’s democratization. In the most serious case, leading an insurrection, he was sentenced to life in prison at the first trial and the appeal is under way, and six other criminal trials remain. If a fine of 1 million won or more becomes final in his election law case, the People Power Party must return 39.7 billion won in campaign costs.

### 1. U.S.–Iran ceasefire near collapse as both sides strike

- **Date**: Jul 7–12 (Tue–Sun)
- **What happened**: On July 7, three merchant ships, including the Qatari LNG carrier Al-Ekhayat, were attacked within a few hours in the Strait of Hormuz. It was the first attack on a Qatari ship since the war began on February 28. The U.S. military treated it as a ceasefire violation by Iran and struck more than 80 targets in Iran that night.   More attacks followed. - **Why it matters**: It was the largest clash since the two countries signed a memorandum of understanding on June 17 to extend the ceasefire by 60 days and restore passage through the strait. Iran claims the right to charge a toll on every ship passing through the strait, and Oman has proposed a new route along its own coast. All three ships attacked were close to the coast of Oman.

### 3. Le Pen launches presidential bid despite appeal conviction

- **Date**: Jul 7–8 (Tue–Wed)
- **What happened**: On July 7, the Paris Court of Appeal upheld the conviction of Marine Le Pen of the French far-right National Rally (RN) for misusing more than €2.8 million in European Parliament funds. The court found Le Pen to be the central figure in a fake jobs scheme that used European Parliament assistants’ salaries to run the party from 2004 to 2016. Of a three-year prison sentence, two years were suspended, and one year is to be served at home with an electronic tag. The fine is €100,000.

  The appeals court set the ban on running for office at 45 months but suspended 30 of them, cutting the actual ban to 15 months. On the evening of the same day, Le Pen announced a run for president and began campaigning under the slogan “For France, a renaissance.” Le Pen said that an appeal to the Court of Cassation would pause the sentence, allowing campaigning without the electronic tag.
- **Why it matters**: By cutting the actual ban on running to 15 months, the appeals court left open a path for the National Rally, which has led in polls, to make Le Pen its candidate in the 2027 presidential election. For more than a year after the first ruling, it was unclear whether Le Pen could run. The first round of the presidential election is in April 2027.

### 1. OpenAI launches GPT-5.6 and shuts down Atlas

- **Date**: Jul 9 (Thu)
- **What happened**: On July 9, OpenAI released a new family of models, GPT-5.6. There are three: the flagship Sol, the mid-tier Terra and the low-cost Luna. - **Why it matters**: GPT-5.6 was announced on June 26, but the Trump administration tried to block its release over fears it could be misused for cyberattacks, then later allowed it. OpenAI is cutting side businesses such as its browser to catch up with Anthropic, which leads in work tools.

### 4. New York Times says OpenAI hid evidence
