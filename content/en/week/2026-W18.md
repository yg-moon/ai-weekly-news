## Korea

### 1. Samsung Biologics workers strike for the first time

- **Date**: Apr 27–May 3 (Mon–Sun)
- **What happened**: On May 1, 2,800 union members at Samsung Biologics began a full strike, the first since the company was founded in 2011. They could not reach agreement with the company on the wage increase and how bonuses are calculated, and they are striking for five days, through the 5th, by taking annual leave and not coming to work. The company estimated that a five-day strike would cause 640 billion won in damage, more than its first-quarter operating profit (580.8 billion won).

  The Samsung Electronics unions, demanding an end to the cap on bonuses and 15% of operating profit as the bonus pool, announced an 18-day general strike from May 21 to June 7. On April 29, the Suwon District Court held its first hearing on the company’s request for an injunction to stop workers from occupying production facilities or getting in the way of safety facilities. The company argued that safety staff, about 5% of all workers, must keep working as normal because of the toxic gases and chemicals used in making chips, while the unions argued that chip plants are not essential public service workplaces.

  President Lee Jae-myung spoke at a meeting with senior secretaries on the 30th. He said, “If some organized workers make excessive and unfair demands to save only themselves and are condemned by the public, it will hurt not only that union but other workers as well.” Choi Seung-ho, head of the Samsung Electronics branch of the Super-Enterprise Union, told members in a group chat that the remark was aimed at the LG Uplus union, which had demanded 30% of operating profit. Choi then apologized after the LG Uplus union protested. Within Choi’s union, more than 2,500 members applied to leave in the 10 days from April 23 to May 2. Most were in the DX division, which makes finished products and was left out of the bonus talks.
- **Why it matters**: Conflicts over who should share how much of the profit from the chip boom broke out all at once: between companies and unions, between unions and the government, and among unions. If the Samsung Electronics unions get what they want, employees in the chip division would receive bonuses of close to 600 million won each. Because a strike could disrupt the world’s memory chip supply, big tech companies abroad began asking about securing supply.

### 2. Kim Keon-hee gets four years on appeal; stock rigging guilty

- **Date**: Apr 28 (Tue)
- **What happened**: On April 28, Criminal Division 15-2 of the Seoul High Court (presiding judge Shin Jong-o) sentenced Kim Keon-hee to four years in prison and a fine of 50 million won, and ordered her to pay 20.94 million won. She was charged with violating the Capital Markets Act and the Political Funds Act and with accepting money to use her influence under the Act on the Aggravated Punishment of Specific Crimes. The sentence is heavier than the one year and eight months she received at the first trial. The charge of manipulating Deutsch Motors stock, on which she was found not guilty at the first trial, was found guilty with her as a co-offender, and receiving a Chanel bag from the Unification Church side was also found guilty. The charge of receiving free polls from Myung Tae-kyun was again found not guilty at the second trial.

  The court found that when Kim handed an account holding 2 billion won to Black Pearl Invest in 2010, her promise to give the firm 40% of the profits may have been payment for pushing up the stock price artificially. A recording in which she asked a securities firm employee, “Aren’t all office calls recorded?” was also used as evidence of guilt. The court said, “The president’s spouse is required to have integrity and morals no less than the president,” and “The defendant instead used that position and threw away the public’s expectations of a president’s spouse.” Kim’s side plans to appeal to the Supreme Court.
- **Why it matters**: Fifteen years after the stock manipulation allegations first came up, a court has for the first time found that Kim took part in the crime. The Seoul Central District Prosecutors’ Office cleared her of this charge in 2024, and the comprehensive special counsel is investigating whether there was outside pressure in that decision.

### 4. High oil price relief payments begin

- **Date**: Apr 27–30 (Mon–Thu)
- **What happened**: On April 27, the first round of relief payments to ease the burden of high oil prices began. The first round covers basic livelihood recipients (550,000 won each) and low-income households just above that level and single-parent families (450,000 won each). People living outside the Seoul metropolitan area or in areas with shrinking populations get 50,000 won more. People can choose to receive the money on a credit or debit card, a prepaid card or local gift certificates, and it expires if not used by August 31. The second round, for 70% of the population, begins on May 18 and pays 100,000 won in the Seoul metropolitan area, 150,000 won outside it and 200,000–250,000 won in areas with shrinking populations.

  Because the money can only be used at stores with annual sales of 3 billion won or less, there was confusion at gas stations from the first day. Only 36% of gas stations could accept it. On the 30th, the government said that from May 1, payments received on credit or debit cards could be used at all gas stations in the area where people live. By the third day, more than 1,526,000 people had applied, over 47% of those eligible for the first round, and 860 billion won had been paid.
- **Why it matters**: The program gives cash-like support to 70% of the population. Although the payments were meant to help with high oil prices, they were hard to use at gas stations, and that design was changed four days after the program began.

### 5. Yoon gets seven years on appeal for blocking his arrest

- **Date**: Apr 29 (Wed)
- **What happened**: On April 29, the Seoul High Court sentenced former President Yoon Suk Yeol to seven years in prison on charges including obstructing officials in their duties by force and abuse of power. Yoon’s side said the ruling was “impossible to accept” and that it would appeal.
- **Why it matters**: A day after Kim Keon-hee, Yoon also received a heavier sentence on appeal. The court said Yoon had used Presidential Security Service officials “like a private army.” Yoon was also sentenced to life in prison at the first trial in the case of leading an insurrection, and that case is now on appeal.

### 4. UAE leaves OPEC and OPEC+

- **Date**: Apr 28 (Tue)
- **What happened**: On April 28, the United Arab Emirates (UAE) announced through its state news agency that it would leave the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) and OPEC+, which also includes Russia and others, as of May 1. The UAE said the decision followed a review of its current and future production capacity and was in its national interest. The UAE is one of OPEC’s three biggest oil producers and had been a member for nearly 60 years, since joining a few years after the group was founded.

  OPEC’s quota had held the UAE’s output to 3.2 million barrels a day, but its production capacity is close to 5 million barrels. The difference equals 1–2% of world oil demand. Because the Strait of Hormuz is mostly blocked, supply will not rise right away. On the day of the announcement, Brent crude was at $111 a barrel, its highest in three weeks.
- **Why it matters**: Saudi Arabia and the UAE are about the only OPEC members with spare production capacity they can use, so the UAE’s exit weakens oil producers’ ability to hold up prices by managing output. When the strait reopens, world oil supply could be larger than when the UAE was a member. Its relationship with Saudi Arabia, which split over issues such as the civil war in Yemen, is also seen as a reason for the exit.

### 5. U.S. Supreme Court sharply narrows the Voting Rights Act

- **Date**: Apr 29 (Wed)
- **What happened**: On April 29, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled 6 to 3 that a 2024 Louisiana map, which created a second congressional district where Black voters are the majority, was an unconstitutional racial gerrymander. The six conservative justices formed the majority.

  The ruling gave a new reading of Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act of 1965. To sue on the grounds that district lines weaken the votes of racial minorities, people must now prove an intent to discriminate, not just a discriminatory result. Justice Samuel Alito wrote that Section 2 should focus on banning intentional racial discrimination. A few hours after the ruling, Florida’s state legislature passed a map creating four new districts that favor Republicans, and there were also calls in Tennessee and Georgia to redraw district lines.
- **Why it matters**: The Voting Rights Act, a key achievement of the civil rights movement, is still on the books, but it has become much harder for racial minorities to challenge unfair district lines in court. At least 15 House districts from Louisiana to North Carolina could disappear, and the number of Black members of Congress could see its largest drop ever.

### 2. Microsoft–OpenAI deal revised, ending exclusivity

- **Date**: Apr 27–28 (Mon–Tue)
