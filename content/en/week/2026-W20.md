## Korea

### 1. Samsung mediation fails; Lee Jae-yong apologizes

- **Date**: May 11–17 (Mon–Sun)
- **What happened**: Samsung Electronics and its unions negotiated for nearly 30 hours over two days, May 11 and 12, in a second round of mediation at the National Labor Relations Commission, but the talks broke down early on the 13th. Choi Seung-ho, head of the Samsung Electronics branch of the Super-Enterprise Union, declared a final breakdown, saying the demands to scrap the cap on bonuses and make the bonus system a fixed rule had not been accepted. The unions demanded that 15% of operating profit go into the bonus pool, that the cap of 50% of annual salary be removed, and that this be fixed as a rule. The company offered to keep its existing profit-sharing bonus (OPI) and, if it became No. 1 in its industry, to pay 10% of operating profit as a special reward with no cap. The unions said 41,000 members would join a general strike on the 21st.

  On the 15th, all of Samsung Electronics’ presidents, including Vice Chairman Jun Young-hyun, issued an apology, saying, “We have caused the public and the government great burden and concern over labor issues.” Chairman Lee Jae-yong, who had been on a business trip to Japan, rushed back on the 16th and apologized to the public, saying, “Union members, members of the Samsung family, we are one body and one family.” It was his first public apology in six years, since May 2020. After the company replaced its lead negotiator as the unions had demanded, both sides agreed to return to mediation on the 18th.

  In an address to the nation on the 17th, Prime Minister Kim Min-seok said, “The talks on the 18th are in effect the last chance to prevent a strike.” He added, “We have no choice but to consider every possible response, including emergency adjustment.” If emergency adjustment is used, strikes and other industrial action are banned for 30 days, and it has been used only four times since 1969. The Korean Confederation of Trade Unions and the Federation of Korean Trade Unions opposed it as a limit on the right to strike.
- **Why it matters**: With one analysis putting the damage from a strike at more than 40 trillion won, the government for the first time raised emergency adjustment, a power that belongs to the labor minister, in an address by the prime minister. The issue was how much of the profit from the chip boom should be shared as bonuses, and under what rules.

### 2. KOSPI tops 8,000 intraday, then plunges 6.1%

- **Date**: May 12–15 (Tue–Fri)

- **Date**: May 14–15 (Thu–Fri)

### 4. Homeplus temporarily closes 37 of its 104 stores

- **Date**: May 11–17 (Mon–Sun)
- **What happened**: Homeplus, which is going through court-led rehabilitation, stopped operating 37 of its 104 hypermarkets nationwide for two months starting May 10. With supplies cut off because of delayed payments to suppliers, it decided to send its remaining goods to 67 core stores. The 3,500 employees at the closed stores will go on leave for two months and receive leave pay of about 70% of their wages. The union protested that mass unemployment had in effect begun.

  On the 17th, Homeplus said that even running the remaining 67 stores had reached its limit, and asked its largest creditor, Meritz Financial, again for an emergency loan to keep operating. It said most of its major assets are pledged to Meritz as collateral, so it cannot borrow on its own, and that it would be hard even to pay this month’s wages on the 21st. The deadline to submit its rehabilitation plan is July 3.
- **Why it matters**: With a third of its hypermarkets closed, employees, store tenants and nearby shoppers were all affected at once. The company itself said that if the remaining stores also stop, it will be hard to keep the rehabilitation process going.

### 5. Rebar missing in 80 columns at GTX-A Samsung Station

- **Date**: May 15–17 (Fri–Sun)
- **What happened**: An underground transit center is being built under Yeongdong-daero in Gangnam, Seoul. Of the 228 columns on its fifth basement level, all 80 in Section 3, built by Hyundai E&C, were found to have only half the main steel bars called for in the design. Bars that should have been placed in pairs were placed one at a time, leaving out about 2,570 bars. This level will hold the platforms of GTX-A and GTX-C, express rail lines for the Seoul area, and the columns hold up the whole structure, which goes 50 m underground. Hyundai E&C said it had misread the drawings.

  The Seoul city government received the contractor’s own report on November 10 last year but did not tell the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport until April 29 this year. The ministry decided to audit how the city managed the project. On the 16th, the city disclosed what had happened and said that reinforcing the columns with steel plates would make them safer than the original design, and that Hyundai E&C would pay the extra cost of about 3 billion won. In the Seoul mayoral race, Democratic candidate Jung Won-o attacked the city for “irresponsible carelessness about safety,” while Mayor Oh Se-hoon hit back that Jung was turning a simple mistake into a political issue.
- **Why it matters**: Construction that did not match the design in a key structure for two Seoul-area express rail lines went unreported to the national government for half a year. GTX-A aims to link Seoul Station and Suseo Station this year, passing through Samsung Station without stopping.

### 4. WHO declares Ebola in Congo and Uganda an emergency

- **Date**: May 15–17 (Fri–Sun)
- **What happened**: On May 17, the World Health Organization (WHO) declared the Ebola outbreak spreading in Ituri province in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo and in Uganda a public health emergency of international concern. It came two days after Congo’s health ministry announced the outbreak on the 15th. By the WHO’s count, Ituri had eight confirmed cases, 246 suspected cases and 80 suspected deaths, and a person who had returned from Ituri to the capital, Kinshasa, also tested positive. In Uganda’s capital, Kampala, one of two confirmed patients who came from Congo died.

  This outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo virus. All but one of Congo’s past outbreaks were of the Zaire type, and there is no approved vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo type. The WHO said the outbreak did not meet the standard for a pandemic emergency and advised countries to step up health screening rather than close their borders. Most patients were in mining towns, where people come and go for work.
- **Why it matters**: A type of the virus with no usable vaccine reached the capitals of both countries right after it was found. Once the WHO declares an international emergency, countries need to put their screening and response systems into action.
